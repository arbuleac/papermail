# SuperSparrow architecture

SuperSparrow is a local-first Mac mail client. Every interaction is a read or write against a local SQLite store; the network never sits between a keystroke and a pixel. This document is the contract every module is built against.

## Product scope for v1

Sparrow's shape, Superhuman's speed:

- Unified inbox across Gmail and Microsoft accounts, with a thin rail: the views as folder tiles at the top, the accounts at its foot to narrow to one account.
- Views: Inbox, Starred, Sent, Drafts, Trash, All mail, Later while something is snoozed, and the Outbox while a message is on its way: every message on its way until the provider has taken it ("Outbox" below). No folders, no labels, ever shown.
- The inbox always shows all of its mail. Gmail's Primary category and Outlook's Focused Inbox are stored (`inbox_section`) only to round-trip provider state; nothing filters or marks rows by them.
- Read conversations as stacked cards; quoted text folded; inline images; attachments with Quick Look, a Download button on each and Download all on a message with two or more (File › Download Attachments, ⇧⌘D), and dragged out to the Finder or any app that takes files, or onto a composer or the quick reply to send on ("Rendering"); find in the open conversation (⌥⌘F, "Find in conversation" below; ⌘F stays the list's search).
- Actions: archive, mark read/unread, star, trash, restore, mark as spam (into the provider's spam folder, which no view lists; "Mark as spam" below), snooze (Later: archived now, back in the Inbox at a time; "Later" below), mute (M: archived now, and its new mail archived as it arrives; "Mute and sender rules" below), and unsubscribe from a mailing list (the link beside the sender, ⇧⌘U), which archives the conversation too, or ends its snooze. Two sender rules, set from a message's menu: Always Archive Mail from This Sender and Block Sender (to the Trash), listed and removed in Settings › Accounts › Senders…. Undo for every action taken in any window, the main window or a reader window, from one history the app keeps (an unsubscribe's undo brings the conversation back; the request stays sent). Rows dragged onto the rail's folders take the same actions: Inbox moves them to the inbox, Trash trashes, Starred stars, All Mail archives; Sent, Drafts, Later and the accounts take nothing, and a drop that would change nothing the list shows is refused. A two-finger swipe on a row archives it (left, the trailing edge) or marks it read or unread (right), as E and U do, and only where they would change it.
- Reply, reply all, forward, new message. Plain composer: bold, italic, underline, links, lists, quotes, attachments, and pictures pasted or dropped into the text, sent as `cid:` parts ("Pictures in the body" below). A signature per account, set in Settings › Accounts. Undo send (five seconds). Send and Archive, the default: a reply or forward archives the conversation it answers as it is queued, and undoing the send brings it back ("Send and Archive"). Send Later: from Send's drop-down (Tomorrow, Monday, Pick a Time…) or ⇧⌘L, in the composer and the quick reply, a message waits on this Mac until its time and goes then while SuperSparrow is open, listed in Drafts as "Sends Mon 09:00 · Cancel" until it goes; a time missed while the app was closed is asked about, never sent silently ("Send Later"). `mailto:` links from other apps open a new message; Settings › General makes SuperSparrow the default mail app. The Services menu's New Message with Selection and Send with SuperSparrow open a new message from text or files selected in any app.
- Full-text search over subject, participants, and body (the snippet until the body is downloaded), as you type, with operators typed into the same field (`from:`, `to:`, `subject:`, `is:unread`, `is:read`, `is:starred`, `has:attachment`, `before:`, `after:`, `in:` a view, quoted phrases, a leading minus; "Search"). When the local results end, the list offers to search the server for more ("Server search").
- Keyboard first: `j`/`k` move, `e` archive, `l` snooze, `m` mute, `s` star, `u` unread, `#` trash, `!` spam, `r` reply, `a` reply all, `f` forward, `c` compose, `/` search, `⌘1`..`⌘9` switch account, `⌘0` unified.
- Unread badge on the Dock icon: All Inboxes' unread count in the rail's words (“99+” above 99, none at zero; "Unread counts"). New-mail notifications; one for a single conversation offers Archive, Mark as Read and Reply, and the summary of several offers none. Settings › General › At login opens SuperSparrow when the user logs in, so they arrive from then on ("Launch at login").
- A bounded store: Settings › General › Keep mail on this Mac (Everything, 1 year, 3 months) with the mailbox's size beside it; older mail keeps its row and searchable text and fetches its full message when opened ("Storage bounds").
- A conversation in a window of its own, for reading beside a composer: ↩ or a double-click on a row (and ⌘O, File › Open in New Window) opens it in a reader window, and Esc closes one whose quick reply holds nothing ("Reader windows").
- Calendar invitations: a message that carries one opens with a card (title, when, where, organizer, guests), its body folded under it, answered with Accept · Tentative · Decline, and Open in Calendar hands the invitation to the Mac's Calendar ("Calendar invitations"). It shows a day and a week beside the mail and writes only answers and time set aside for mail.
- Day and week: every account's main calendar beside the mail, each event in its account's ink: a day column to the right of the reader, on by default (D), and the week in the reader's place (W), neither ever scrolling. B, or a row dropped on a day or the week, sets 30 minutes aside for that mail in its account's calendar, with a toast and Undo ("Calendar").
- Print: File › Print… (⌘P) prints the open conversation's messages, every one open and oldest first, as the reader shows each one (its images, its folded quoted text), on light paper whatever the app's appearance, through the system print panel, which also saves a PDF ("Print").

- Help me write (⌘J, ⇧⌘J to say how): a draft of the reply being written, in place in the quick reply or a reply composer, from the whole conversation (newest first, older messages shortened) and the user's notes, by the provider chosen in Settings › AI (Claude with an API key or the user's Claude Code, OpenAI with an API key or the user's Codex). Off by default; turned on in Settings › AI past a privacy sheet; nothing reaches a provider unless the user presses Write or Check ("AI").

Out of scope for v1: rules beyond the two sender rules, S/MIME, server drafts, on-premises Exchange, IMAP, multiple windows beyond compose and reader windows. What comes after v1, and in what order, is `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

## Modules

```
SSDesign (Quire tokens, components)
        ▲ draws with
SuperSparrowApp (AppKit, WebKit)  ──reads──▶  MailStoring (SQLite)  ◀──writes──  SyncEngine (actors)
        │ commands                                                                   │
        └──────────────▶  MailClient (facade)  ─────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                                                      │
                                                                  MailProvider ◀──────┘
                                                               ┌──────┴──────┐
                                                        GmailProvider    GraphProvider
                                                               └──────┬──────┘
                                                          AccessTokenProvider (OAuth, Keychain)

SuperSparrowApp ──Write──▶  AIWriter (SSCore/AI, the service)  ──▶  AIProvider (SSCore/Ports)
                                                                    ┌───────────┴───────────┐
                                                     SSAI: Anthropic, OpenAI (URLSession)   Claude Code, Codex (Process)
```

- **SSCore/Model**: value types shared by everything. Already written; change only by agreement.
- **SSCore/Ports**: `MailProvider`, `MailStoring`, `MailClient`, `TokenStore`, `AccessTokenProvider`, sanitizer output; `CalendarProvider` and `CalendarStoring`, which `MailStoring` refines ("Calendar"). Already written.
- **SSCore/Store**: `SQLiteMailStore: MailStoring` using GRDB `DatabasePool` (WAL). Schema in `docs/DATA-MODEL.md`.
- **SSCore/Sync**: `AccountSyncer` (two lanes per account: the `LiveLane` actor owns the change cursor, replays the outbox and polls; the `BackfillLane` actor downloads older mail and missing bodies behind it; and, for a provider with a calendar, the `CalendarLane` actor, which sends the time set aside and removed on this Mac and reads the account's calendar, see "Calendar"), `QuotaBudget` (one per account, paces the backfill by `ProviderQuota`, see "Quota budget"), `OperationRunner` (outbox replay), `SendRunner` (undo window then send; Send Later at its time, "Send Later"), `SnoozeRunner` (brings snoozed conversations back at their time, "Later"), `Hydrator` (bodies: a live path for new mail, a paced path for the backfill, on-demand fetches), `AttachmentCache` (attachment files on disk, at most 1 GB; a miss downloads through the provider into a partial file in one folder, swept before a run's first download since an interrupted run leaves its files there, charged as live spend, and concurrent callers share the download and its progress through an `AttachmentProgressRelay`), `StorageSweeper` (the Keep mail window and the store's housekeeping, see "Storage bounds"), `ServerSearcher` (a search of the server for mail this Mac lacks, see "Server search"), `MailEngine: MailClient` (facade wiring these together).
- **SSCore/Transport**: what both providers' transports share for downloads: `HTTPDownload` (one request whose 2xx body is written to a file as it arrives, through a data task with a task delegate, so a test session's stub `URLProtocol` drives it; an error reply's body comes back in memory for the transport's own status mapping) and `DownloadProgressMeter` (bytes into fractions that only rise and stop at 0.99 until the caller says the file is complete; measured against Content-Length unless the body is compressed, otherwise against the caller's estimate, and silent with neither).
- **SSCore/Auth**: `OAuthFlow` (PKCE, loopback redirect on 127.0.0.1, system browser), `KeychainTokenStore: TokenStore`, `TokenManager: AccessTokenProvider`, `ClientConfigurationStore` (reads `oauth-clients.json`).
- **SSCore/Render**: `HTMLSanitizer` producing `SanitizedBody` with SwiftSoup, plus `QuoteDetector` and `TextExtractor`.
- **SSCore/Calendar**: our own small iCalendar reader and writer ("Calendar invitations"): `CalendarInvitationReader` (an invitation's facts out of iCalendar text), `RecurrenceRule` (a repeat rule in words, never expanded), `CalendarTimeZones` (IANA, vendor-prefixed and Windows zone names), `InvitationCalendarWriter` (the iMIP reply, and a calendar file written from facts). For the day column and the week ("Calendar"): `CalendarWindow` (the days each calendar is kept for), `CalendarFit` (a day or a week laid out with no scrolling, its free half hours, its day cells and an invitation's overlaps; pure, no AppKit) and `MailTimeDetector` (the times a mail's text names, found once when its body is stored).
- **SSGmail**: Gmail REST API client. Bootstrap = `users.messages.list` newest first with `-in:spam -in:trash -in:chats` (chats are hidden from All mail in Gmail too), hydrated via batch `messages.get?format=metadata` (the address, subject, date and threading headers, plus `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post`, which cost nothing more: a get is priced per call) in batches of 10 parts: a part costs 20 units, so a batch is 200 units rather than 1,000 of the 6,000 a user may spend a minute. Throttled parts (429, or 403 `rateLimitExceeded`/`userRateLimitExceeded`) are sent again on their own after the largest part's Retry-After (or the refill time Google names), otherwise after 1, 2 and 4 s, keeping the parts already answered; such a wait is sat out inline up to 60 s once the call has fetched messages, and with nothing fetched yet a wait over 10 s is reported at once. A Retry-After over 60 s, or parts still throttled after the schedule, raise `ProviderError.throttled`, which discards that call's fetched messages. 404 parts are skipped, other failed or unanswered parts are fetched one by one, and a 401 part refreshes the token and resends the batch. A message that fails on its own even then is left out rather than thrown, so it never fails a changes call after its history was paged (holding the cursor on every poll), a bootstrap, a backfill page or a search, and `lookUpMetadata(for:)` says which and how: refused (a 400, a 403 that is not a rate limit, a reply that does not decode), or failing (a 500, Gmail's passing backendError). A changes call keeps what the store has for either, since only a 404 says Gmail no longer has a message; a failing one rides in the cursor (`GmailHistoryCursor.recheck`, absent while empty, so a healthy cursor stays a bare history id) and a call at least 30 s after its last try asks for it again (`GmailProvider.recheckSpacing`; the calls of one `hasMore` burst do not count), up to 3 tries, so new mail is not lost to a blip. A bootstrap names both in `BootstrapPage.unreadableIDs`, which a reconcile counts as listed, and its cursor carries the failing ones for the first polls; a backfill page whose metadata failed for some messages goes again on later turns asking for those alone (`SyncTuning.failingPageTries`), then moves on. The network (a 502, 503 or 504 included), a throttle or a refused token on that fetch still fails the call. A reconcile lists the ids of Trash and Spam apart (`mailOutsideEnumeration`: `in:trash OR in:spam` with `includeSpamTrash`, 500 ids a page) so its sweep keeps what the enumeration leaves out but the store holds: a stored message already in Trash or Spam is marked seen as it is, and one the store shows elsewhere (trashed or marked spam on the phone during the gap) is refreshed from its metadata first, so it does not keep its old place, the Inbox say, for good (`OutsideEnumeration`). The backfill listing (`backfillListing(page:limit:)`) is one `users.messages.list` call with the same `q` and `maxResults` as bootstrap, ids only (5 units a page); a bootstrap page token continues it. Gmail's per-user quota refusals carry no Retry-After header, so the refill time is read from "Retry after <RFC 3339>" in Google's error message; a time already past counts as unknown (nil). Each throttled response, or group of batch parts, is logged once at notice in the "gmail" category: HTTP status, Google's reasons and status, the request kind and the retry time public, the message text private. Changes = `users.history.list` from the `historyId` captured by `users.getProfile` before the head page; the live lane stores it as the cursor right after that page, not when the enumeration ends. Bodies via `messages.get?format=full`, MIME tree walked for text/html, text/plain, attachments and inline parts; the list headers are read from it too, so a message stored before the metadata asked for them learns them when its body is fetched again. Attachments the user opens (`downloadAttachment`) stream: the `messages.attachments.get` reply, a JSON object whose `data` is the file in base64url, is written to a file beside the cache's partial file as it arrives (`GmailHTTPClient.download`: the same token retry, status mapping and throttle log as every other request), then `GmailAttachmentDecoder` maps that file, finds the top-level `data` string and decodes it a slice at a time into the attachment cache, so neither the reply nor the file sits in memory whole. Its progress counts the reply's bytes against its Content-Length or, since Google usually sends it chunked, against the base64 length of the listed size plus the JSON around it, and stops at 99% until the file is decoded and written; with neither length it reports nothing. The oversized text parts a body fetch resolves still come in one response (`attachment(messageID:attachmentID:)`). Send builds RFC 5322 MIME (multipart/alternative inside multipart/mixed; the HTML alternative becomes multipart/related, the HTML first, when the message carries parts with a Content-ID: the composer's pictures and a forward's inline parts, each `Content-Disposition: inline`) base64url-encoded into `messages.send`, with `threadId` when replying; messages over 1 MiB of MIME go through the media upload endpoint (`uploadType=multipart`) instead because Gmail caps JSON request bodies. Changes map to `messages.batchModify` label edits (`GmailLabelEdit`; Mark as spam adds SPAM and removes INBOX and TRASH, its undo the reverse). Sender rules are Gmail filters (`users.settings.filters`, behind `gmail.settings.basic`; "Rules" below).
- **SSGraph**: Microsoft Graph client. Bootstrap = `/me/messages?$orderby=receivedDateTime desc&$select=...` paging, excluding Deleted Items and Junk by parentFolderId. Changes = per-folder `/me/mailFolders/{id}/messages/delta` for inbox, sentitems, drafts, archive, deleteditems, junkemail and every other folder at any depth, found by walking the tree from `/me/mailFolders` (hidden folders left out) through `/childFolders` wherever a folder reports `childFolderCount` > 0, a level at a time down to 16 levels (`GraphProvider.maxFolderDepth`: far deeper than people file mail, it only stops a broken listing that keeps handing back new children from walking for ever, and a folder below it is left to the bootstrap listing and a reconcile), each folder kept once and its children listed once, so a duplicate or a cycle in the listing ends the walk, and a folder that answers 404 when its children are asked for (deleted since its parent was listed) is skipped with them, as the next walk would find. The walk costs the top-level listing plus one listing per folder with children, a request per page of 200 folders, sent one after another, so it is spread over calls and kept in the cursor (`GraphCursor.walk`: the folders found and the listings still to send, with the nextLink of one a step stopped partway through): the call that starts one looks up the system folders and sends the top level's first page, and each call after sends at most 5 listing pages (`GraphProvider.maxWalkPagesPerCall`, about a second; counted in pages, not listings, so a folder holding thousands of others is listed over several calls), so a tree with 60 folders that have children takes twelve polls and no poll, new inbox mail and all, waits for a whole tree. A listing that fails other than with a 404 holds up neither the step nor the poll: a throttle, a refused token, a cancellation or a reset fails the call as its next request would, and anything else (a 500, access denied, a 503 or 504 without Retry-After, a page that does not decode) ends the step with the listing left first in the walk for the next call to try again; after 3 calls (`GraphProvider.maxWalkAttempts`) the walk gives up on that listing and goes on with the rest (`GraphFolderWalk.gaveUp`). A walk starts at bootstrap, which takes its first two steps before the head page (the second only if the first sent no failed listing) and leaves the rest to the polls, and with the daily folder refresh (`GraphCursor.folderRefreshInterval`, counted from the walk's start by the cursor's `foldersResolvedAt`), never on an ordinary poll; the call that starts one keeps the system folder ids it had when a lookup fails. A cursor without the `folderScope` marker was listed two levels deep by an earlier version, so its walk is due on its first poll rather than a day after that listing. A folder the walk finds, at any depth, gets its delta and starts seeding at once (a folder made in Outlook is found by the next walk). One it did not find stays on its delta link, listed in the cursor's `missed`, and is dropped when the next walk misses it too: a walk spread over polls can miss a folder moved meanwhile out of a listing still to come into one already sent, and a deleted folder usually goes sooner, when its delta answers 404. Either way the page names the folder (`ChangesPage.goneFolders`): a folder deleted for good in Outlook takes its mail with it and no delta reports that, so the live lane queues what the store keeps in it (`queueLookups(accountID:inFolders:)`, the `queued_lookups` table, written before the cursor moves past the report; the scan for the folder's mail runs on a reader, since in a 300,000-message mailbox it held the writer 63 ms, and only the inserts take the writer) and looks the queue up a few per poll (`SyncTuning.queuedLookupsPerPoll`, 20, and no more than the live share has room for, after the poll's own pages and bodies) through `lookUpMetadata(for:)`: what Graph no longer has goes, and so does what it refuses (its 403 for an item deleted for good, as a delta's removal takes it); what was moved out before the purge takes its new place; what the service fails stays queued and is asked again a minute later (`queuedLookupRetryDelay`), up to 5 tries, then is left as stored. A folder of thousands is so worked through without holding new mail back or outrunning the mailbox's limit, and a failure of the whole lookup (the network, the service) fails no poll and loses nothing done; a throttle or a refused token fails the poll as any request's would. A walk that gave up on a listing drops nothing and adds nothing to `missed`, since it did not look under every folder. A folder found late misses nothing, since its first round lists its whole content. Each delta is seeded without a `$filter` (the only filter message delta accepts, `receivedDateTime ge`, also limits which messages the delta tracks, so reads, moves and deletes of older mail would never be reported); inbox, sentitems and drafts, plus any folder partway through a round on a nextLink, are drained round-robin on every poll until each reaches its deltaLink or about `limit` items are collected; every other folder already on its deltaLink (a quiet folder) waits for a full visit, which starts at most once every 30 s (`GraphCursor.fullVisitInterval`; the cursor keeps when the latest started as `fullVisitAt`, and a cursor without it starts one on its next call) and is spread over calls: each call polls the next quiet folders in id order after the last one the visit polled (`fullVisitAfter`, so a folder added or dropped meanwhile shifts nothing), 2 for every second since the last call (`polledAt`) and never more than 20 (`GraphCursor.quietVisitsPerSecond`, `maxQuietVisitsPerCall`). So a quiet poll is never a heavy page for the live lane (the priority folders, 20 quiet ones, a seed page and a walk step stay under the 34 requests that make one, "Live lane" above) and the quiet folders' cost never holds back the inbox's next poll, however large the tree. At the live lane's 5 s interval a mailbox with 40 folders spends about 1,100 of Outlook's 10,000 requests per 10 minutes per mailbox instead of 4,800, each quiet folder still polled every 30 s, and one with 200 folders about 1,600, each quiet folder polled about every 100 s; a visit of all 200 in one call cost 200 requests, and the live lane held each next poll, the inbox's with it, back about 36 s to pay for that. Polled every 30 s in the background, a visit of more than 20 quiet folders takes more than one poll. Every other folder still in its first round gets one seed page per poll (`maxSeedPagesPerCall` = 1), one folder at a time in folder order, and a seeding folder's nextLink alone does not set `hasMore`, so new inbox mail is never held behind a re-listing of the mailbox. A folder other than the inbox whose delta request fails with anything but a throttle, a refused token, a cancellation or a reset (a 5xx, the network, a 403 access denied, a 400 on its stored link, a page that does not decode) keeps its place and waits for the next call while the others sync, counted in the cursor's `folderFailures` (logged with the status only); a seeding folder that failed yields the seed page to those that did not (`nextSeed`), so one folder whose page keeps failing never holds every later one back from its first round. After 3 calls in a row (`GraphProvider.maxFolderFailures`) it is parked: asked only when a full visit reaches it, seed page and all, and one Graph refused (rather than the service failing) starts a fresh round, since its stored link may be what Graph refuses. Answering clears its count. The inbox failing, or every folder, still fails the call, so the account shows it is not syncing. The first round lists each folder once (a non-priority folder's over several polls, a page per poll), which also repairs anything the offset-paged bootstrap skipped. A removal a delta reports is looked up by id in `$batch` before it counts as a deletion, since a move out of a folder reads the same, and so is a change a delta reports with only what changed (a read flag). Only Graph's answer about the message decides (`GraphProvider+Changes.lookUpReported`): found is an upsert; unknown (404) is gone; refused for that one message (a 400 or 403, a reply that does not decode) takes a removal as gone, which is what Graph's 403 ErrorAccessDenied for a deleted item means, and keeps what the store has for a change; a part or a whole `$batch` the service fails (a 5xx, the network) says nothing about the message, which keeps what the store has and is looked up again on a call at least 30 s after its last try (the cursor's `recheck`, with the tries that failed it, and `recheckAt`; `GraphProvider.recheckSpacing`, so the calls of one `hasMore` burst, which come within a second, neither ask nor count) up to 3 tries (`maxLookupCalls`), then is left as stored. The delta links move on meanwhile, so an outage that fails lookups holds no new mail back and deletes nothing that was only moved (a snooze's archive would take the snooze with it); a throttle or a refused token still fails the call. Each page reports the requests its call sent (`requestCount`, tallied by the transport: every attempt, a `$batch` as its parts), which the live lane is charged. The opaque cursor is a resumable record of each folder's place: a JSON map of folder id to deltaLink, plus each folder's in-progress nextLink, the well-known folder ids, when the folder list was last resolved, a folder walk under way, the folders the last walk missed, when the latest full visit started and how far it has got, when the last call ran, the messages to look up again and the folders failing in a row (both absent when none). The backfill listing is the protocol's default over bootstrap, since a Graph page carries its metadata inline. Bodies via `/me/messages/{id}?$select=body,uniqueBody,internetMessageHeaders` with `Prefer: outlook.body-content-type="html"` (the headers are where `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` come from: list and delta calls cannot select them, so a Microsoft message learns them with its body) and `/attachments` (Content-IDs selected as `microsoft.graph.fileAttachment/contentId`, since the base attachment type has none). An attachment's raw bytes stream from `/me/messages/{id}/attachments/{id}/$value` into the attachment cache as they arrive (`GraphHTTPClient.download`: the same token retry, request tally and status mapping as every other request), counted against Content-Length or, without one, the listed size (Graph's listed size can run over the file's, so a stated length wins); an attachment with no `$value` (an item or reference attachment: a 404 or another server error) falls back to its base64 `contentBytes` in JSON, in one response. Send via `/me/sendMail` while attachments fit its 4 MB request, otherwise via a draft (`POST /me/messages`); a part with a content id (the composer's pictures, a forward's inline parts) is a `fileAttachment` with `isInline` and `contentId`, in sendMail's JSON, posted to the draft, or named so in its upload session; replies via `/me/messages/{id}/createReply` then PATCH then `/send` so Outlook threads them. Draft attachments of 3 MB and more go through `createUploadSession` in 3.125 MiB chunks; a draft whose filling fails is deleted so retries leave no orphans. Changes map to PATCH `isRead`/`flag` and POST `/move` (to a well-known folder name: `archive`, `inbox`, `deleteditems`, and `junkemail` for Mark as spam). Batch with `$batch` (20 per call). An apply sends every batch whatever a part of an earlier one answered (a throttle or a refused token stops the rest; a request that failed whole too), sends the parts the service failed again on their own after 1 s and then 2 s (`applyRetryDelays`), and throws what it still owes: the error alone when nothing landed, otherwise `PartialApplyError` naming the messages it did not change, with the gravest error met (refused token, throttle with the longest Retry-After, a failed request, the service, a refusal). 429, and 503 with Retry-After, map to `throttled` with the Retry-After value, and each throttled response is logged once at notice in the "graph" category (status, Graph error code, Retry-After, nothing about the mail); a 502, and a 503 or 504 without Retry-After, is a network error (the service failing for now: the network backoff, "slow to respond", never an operation's give-up), as Gmail's 502, 503 and 504 are, while a 500 stays a server error. `metadata(for:)` leaves out a message Graph will not return on its own as it leaves out an unknown one, so one such hit never fails a server search; `lookUpMetadata(for:)` says which it left out without knowing them gone. Sender rules are inbox rules (`/me/mailFolders/inbox/messageRules`, behind `MailboxSettings.ReadWrite`; "Rules" below).
- **SSCore support folders** (`ListSupport/`, `ConversationSupport/`, `ComposeSupport/`, `ShellSupport/`): the pure, testable halves of the app's surfaces (row text and layout, the server row's words and rules, the reader's render payload and shell, the printout built from them, reply recipients and drafts, the sync line, undo order, toasts' words, which rail folder takes which dragged rows). `Model/AccountLabels` is the one place an account's monogram, tile letter, name and From title are derived, so every surface shows the same ones.
- **SSDesign**: the Quire design runtime and every component, as a library the app links. See "Design" below.
- **SuperSparrowApp**: `AppDelegate`, `MainWindowController` (NSSplitViewController: account rail, message list, conversation), `MessageListViewController` (NSTableView, fixed rows, manual layout, cached attributed strings; a search ends with the server row, run by its `ServerSearchController`; a two-finger swipe slides a row's content over the button the list draws behind it, `RowSwipeController`, "Swipe" under Data flow), `ConversationViewController` (warm `WKWebView` pool, `ss-cid` scheme handler, quick reply), `ConversationPrinter` (File › Print…, "Print"), `ComposeWindowController`, `ReaderWindowController` (a conversation in a window of its own, "Reader windows"), `MailUndoCenter` (the app's undo history and its one toast), `KeyboardRouter`, `SettingsWindowController` (SwiftUI), `NewMailNotifier` and `NewMailNotificationResponder`, the Dock badge (`DockBadge`, below), and, in debug builds, the Design Gallery (`Design/`, below). The account scope (All Inboxes or one account, chosen in the rail, the title menu or with ⌘0…⌘9) is remembered across launches: the main window saves it in UserDefaults through `AppSettings` whenever it changes (`accountScope`: `unified` or the account id; the demo keeps its own under `accountScope.demo`, so trying the demo never changes the real choice) and restores it at its first read that finds accounts, before the list's first query, so the list never shows All Inboxes first (the tile is scrolled into view only when the rail appears, at the window's final size, so the stack opens as choosing that account there would leave it). A saved account that is gone opens All Inboxes and the saved value is cleared; a demo seeded after its window opens (its first launch, or after its data was wiped) always ends that way, since the seeder gives every account a new id. Removing the scoped account returns to All Inboxes and saves that. It is not a setting and has no UI of its own.

A kept draft's archive cannot hold the bytes (and should not): `ComposeDraftStore` archives the body with each picture's `.attachment` removed (`ComposeInlineImages.detached`), keeping its character and content id, writes each picture's bytes as `image-<content id>` beside the record, which lists them (`ComposeDraftRecord.images`, absent in older records), and on reading puts each picture back by its content id (`attached`, dropping a character whose file is gone); each save removes files the draft no longer names. The Drafts snippet leaves pictures out and the reader's draft sheet shows `[image: name]`, built from the record and the archived body alone (`ComposeDraftStore.readerBody`), never reading or decoding a picture on the conversation-open path. - **Unsubscribe**: `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369) and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (RFC 8058) are captured at sync time and stored on the message as sent (`messages.list_unsubscribe`, `list_unsubscribe_post`, schema v14, docs/DATA-MODEL.md): Gmail from every metadata fetch and the full body fetch, Graph from the body fetch only. Headers never change, so a write that carries none (a Graph listing) keeps the stored pair. Mail stored before v14 has none until fetched again; nothing fetches it back for this. `UnsubscribeMethod` (SSCore/Model, pure) reads the pair as the one way to ask: the angle-bracketed URIs in order, comments and folding white space tolerated, mailto, https and http only; an RFC 8058 one-click request when the post header is exactly `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` and an https URI is offered, else the first mailto naming exactly one recipient, else the first https (or http) page; a URI with a user name or password in it is skipped. The render payload carries one flag per message (`unsubscribe`), so the reader's shell shows the link beside the sender (spec §5.14) without deciding anything; the More menu's item, Message › Unsubscribe (⇧⌘U, the open conversation's latest message that offers it, read from the reader's messages, so validating it reads no store) and the same item in the rows' and messages' context menu (there only when the open conversation offers it) take the same path. One click, no alert: `MainWindowController.unsubscribe(from:)` computes the conversation's archive as E would (`MailActionRecord`) and calls `MailClient.unsubscribe`, which stores the archive and, for a one-click or mailto method, an `unsubscribe` operation (`MetadataChange.unsubscribe`, carrying the method and URI, no targets) in one transaction (`MailStoring.enqueue(_:)` for several operations). A snoozed conversation (in Later, or a search's result) is archived already, so its snooze ends instead, as E ends it in Later, in the request's transaction (`MailStoring.setSnoozes(_:enqueueing:at:)`): the list's mail never comes back to the Inbox at its time. A page is opened at once with `NSWorkspace`; no engine is involved and only the archive (or the snooze's end) is stored. The toast says what became of the request and offers Undo for the archive (“Unsubscribe sent · Archived”, spec §5.30), or for the snooze's end, which puts the snooze back; its undo group never coalesces (an E in Later after it, named Archive like it, starts a toast of its own), and Undo brings the conversation back while the request stays sent, which the undo's confirmation says (“Undid Archive · Unsubscribe stays sent”). While that toast is up, the same request asked again (⇧⌘U again where the archived row stays listed) does nothing, so it is never queued twice and the toast keeps its Undo; the shell ignores a double-click's second click on the link, which may land on the next conversation's link once the archive has moved the reader on. `OperationRunner` sends unsubscribe operations after the account's other operations, each on its own through `Unsubscriber`: they change no message, so they never wait behind, or hold back, the archive or anything else. The one-click POST runs off the live lane's cycle on a task of its own (`Unsubscriber.detach`), its row written ahead past the request's whole timeout so no later drain sends it twice, so a slow sender's server never holds back a poll; stopping the engine cancels it and leaves the row for the next run (`stopAndWait` waits for it to write its row). The mailto request is the provider's own send and is waited for like any other provider call. The one-click POST (body `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`) goes on a session made for the request and invalidated after it: ephemeral, no cookie storage and none sent or accepted, no credential storage (an authentication challenge other than the server's certificate is declined, never answered: a password challenge ends in its 401, a client-certificate request goes on without one, and default handling, which could answer Negotiate with the Mac's Kerberos sign-in, is not used), no cache, 15 s per request and 30 s in all, redirects followed only to https. The mailto request goes through the provider's own send (`MailProvider.send`) from the conversation's account, to the link's one recipient (the mail leaves silently with words the sender chose, so a link naming several recipients is not used and its Cc and Bcc are ignored), its subject or “Unsubscribe”, its body as plain text, with no undo window and outside the outbox's sending count; the provider files it in Sent and the next poll brings it. The outbox's send path is not used because it gives up on a network error (the provider may have delivered), where an unsubscribe waits for the network like any operation. Offline, a dropped connection, the provider's throttle on a mailto send, or a stop (told by the task's own cancellation; a cancelled request otherwise counts as a failure): tried again on the operation backoff, never given up. A 2xx (or a sent mail) confirms it; a 4xx other than 408 and 429, a redirect away from https, or a provider's refusal drops it quietly; a 5xx, the sender's 408 or 429 (its `Retry-After` honoured, up to `SyncTuning.userRetryAfterCap`), a timeout or an unreachable server is tried again until `SyncTuning.maxOperationAttempts`, then dropped quietly, so a sender's server that keeps throttling is not asked for ever. Dropped is deleted, never written as failed, so no row shows a failure and nothing is given back (the request changed nothing locally). A refusal is logged at notice with its status and no URI; the URI, which can identify the user, is logged at debug only. The demo's senders are made up, so the runner sends no POST for a demo account.

Its keys go through a `KeyboardRouter` of its own (E, L, #, !, ⇧I, S, U, M, Z, R, A, F, C; J, K, ↩ and → pass to the page, and ← too unless the window's toast or find bar takes it, as Esc's path does (`KeyCommand.back`); /, the G-sequences and ? act in the main window, which comes forward), its menus through the same selectors as the main window's, answered for its conversation (Mark as Spam, Mute, the sender rules, Download Attachments, Print… and the find commands included; View's mailboxes and accounts, ⌘0 to ⌘9, bring the main window forward on them, as the G-sequences do), and Edit › Undo through `ReaderWindow`. The place holds its window weakly, so no step ever reaches a window that has gone. **Deep undo** (spec §6.1): the history keeps the last 50 groups (`MailActionUndoHistory.capacity`; the 51st drops the oldest, and the main window then lets go of what it kept beside them, `forgetGroupsGone`). ## Data flow

1. **Add account**: `MailClient.addAccount` → `OAuthFlow` opens the system browser → loopback receives the code → tokens go to Keychain → provider `profile()` → `Account` row → engine starts the account's two lanes.
2. **Head page and cursor first**: an account without a change cursor gets one from its newest page. The live lane fetches `bootstrap(page: nil)` (100 messages, with the cursor the provider captured before listing), saves a `BootstrapState` holding that cursor and no page, upserts the head in one transaction, saves the state again with its next page, then the cursor, and fetches the newest bodies. A crash or a failed write anywhere after the first save leaves a state whose cursor the next launch adopts, so polling covers every head row (even one newer mail has pushed off a later head) and the backfill lists from the top; so does a download the single-loop engine left in progress, except that its backfill resumes at the saved page. The first poll brings in everything since. However large the mailbox, new mail flows from the first poll on (no gap: the cursor predates the listing, and duplicates are idempotent).
3. **Live lane**: replays the outbox, then polls `changes(since:)` until `hasMore == false`, saving the cursor after each page: every 5 s while the app is frontmost and every 30 s otherwise (the schedule runs on the monotonic clock, so setting the Mac's clock back cannot postpone a poll), plus on `syncNow`, activation, waking from sleep and the network coming back. Only Sync Now and Reconnect (`syncNow`, `WakeReason.poll`) try a parked account's token again; the automatic checks (`checkForMail`, `WakeReason.check`) leave it parked, so a refused keychain's prompt never comes back with nobody asking, and the token provider, shared by every account, is never held on that prompt. It then fetches the bodies of the newest 20 messages new to the store or back from Spam (not other stored ones whose labels or folder changed, and not spam), 2 at a time and only as many as its share of the quota pays for (each waiting, 10 s at most, while the reader waits for a body: "Opening a message"), and leaves the rest to the backfill; after a head page (a new account, or a reset) it fetches the newest 20 whatever the share holds. It never waits on the backfill, so a throttled download cannot hold back new mail, archive or star; what it can put off it fits to its own share of the quota ("Quota budget"): after a changes page that said `hasMore` or was heavy (more than a tenth of the share: about ten Gmail messages, or 34 Graph requests), the next page, of the same poll or the next, waits outside the cycle until the share covers another page like it, so a catch-up larger than the share goes on at about a page a minute on Gmail, and so do the polls of a busy mailbox that each bring a heavy page, while the outbox keeps moving. After a light page the next poll's first page goes whatever the share owes (the bodies of an opened conversation count against it), so the steady poll (2 Gmail units) and a few new messages never wait, and nor does Sync Now unless a run or a heavy page came just before it. While a backfill runs, it marks each page's upserts and deletes (`markLiveChanges`) before writing them. A rejected cursor (`resetRequired`) no longer wipes anything: the live lane takes a fresh head page and cursor and only then replaces the old backfill (its state first, with nowhere to resume from, then its marks) with a reconciling one. A reset whose head fails leaves the running backfill as it was, and one whose writes fail leaves a reconcile that lists from the top before it sweeps, never one resumed with half its marks. Only a cycle that polled moves its phase: a user action during a failing poll's backoff runs the outbox alone and leaves "Couldn't sync", "Rate limited" and the retry countdown as they were. Its throttles and failures back off up to 60 s (a provider's Retry-After is honoured up to that cap: a time hours away is more often a skewed clock than advice); a throttle on any of its requests (a head or changes page, the outbox, a body it fetches) also pauses the backfill, which it never waits on ("Quota budget"); and a refused token or keychain parks both lanes until Sign in or Sync Now. After each full cycle that caught up it tells the account's calendar lane, which reads when its turn is due and never holds the next poll back ("Calendar").
4. **Backfill lane**: downloads the rest behind the live lane, paced by the account's quota budget. One listing page at a time from the saved page token (Gmail: ids only, then metadata ten at a time for the ids the store lacks, each chunk written as it arrives; Graph: metadata inline), written with `writeBackfillMessages`, which skips whatever the live lane wrote or deleted meanwhile, so a page fetched before a live change can neither undo it nor bring back a deleted message (docs/DATA-MODEL.md, "Backfill marks"). The state is saved after every page, so a quit resumes there, and a stored page token the provider rejects restarts the listing from the top. Each page is followed by one paced body batch (inbox first, newest first), a page that failed too unless the failure is one the batch would meet (a throttle, a refused token or keychain, the network, a park), so a page that keeps failing never stops the bodies; once the listing completes, the lane fetches the remaining bodies, then sleeps until woken or for 5 minutes. None of its requests goes out while the reader waits for a body of the account ("Opening a message"). Every body batch is followed by one local batch of design facts for bodies stored before v3. At launch the lane waits for the live lane's first cycle, so a token or keychain prompt is asked for once, and it looks at the park before every request, so a turn under way stops once the account is parked. A reconciling backfill also refreshes stored rows and records every id it lists; when complete it removes the stored messages the provider no longer listed, except those a live operation targets, and those only if the provider, asked just before (`lookUpMetadata(for:)`), no longer has them: their deletion sat in the expired history, so no poll would report it. One the provider will not return on its own (Graph's 403 for one message, a part the service fails) is kept like any other target rather than failing the reconcile on every retry. A throttle pauses this lane, and so does a throttle on anything that spends the live share ("Quota budget"): no request of this lane goes out until the pause ends, one already waiting for its units included, and a throttle on one fetch of a body batch stops the fetches of that batch that have not gone out, so the batch reports it while the pause runs. The pause shows as `AccountSyncStatus.backfillPausedUntil` from the moment a request of this lane meets it until that request goes out, so a pause after the download has finished shows nowhere. Its errors never change the live phase. A failure backoff ignores wakes, except a replaced backfill and bodies of new mail the live lane hands over after a poll that went through (the network is back).
5. **User action**: UI calls `MailClient.perform` (from a key, a menu, a reader button, rows dropped on a rail folder, or a swipe on a row, which all take the same path in `MainWindowController`) → `store.enqueue(operation)` applies the change to the targets and inserts the operation in one transaction → observations fire → `OperationRunner` replays pending operations in order per account with exponential backoff, a call per 50 targets (`SyncTuning.maxApplyTargets`); a slice the provider refused in part or whole does not keep the later slices from going out (only a throttle, a refused token, the network or a cancellation does), and an operation the provider took in part keeps only the targets still owed (`PartialApplyError`), so a retry sends those alone and a give-up puts back only those; `confirmed` operations are deleted; `uncertain` ones (timeouts) are left for a poll to settle once it has reported every target and their last attempt is 2 minutes old (`SyncTuning.uncertainSettleAge`: past the 60 s a request may take, and below the 300 s backoff cap, since the outbox retries a due operation just before each poll) (docs/DATA-MODEL.md, "Confirmed operations"), and retried meanwhile. An attempt counts towards the give-up (`SyncTuning.maxOperationAttempts`) only when the provider answered it: on the network or a throttle the count stops where the backoff reaches its cap, so an hour and a half offline never leaves the first server error after it to give the change up; a token that could not be had (the token endpoint failing) is retried the same way and never given up, and a keychain the user refuses parks the account at once, before the poll asks the keychain again, without counting the attempt. An invitation's answer is never settled by a poll, which does not report it, so it retries until it is confirmed or given up. Answering an invitation is the same path (`MailClient.answerInvitation` builds the operation from the stored invitation), with one difference: before any replay the runner asks the provider, without a request, whether the change needs a permission the account has not granted (`MailProvider.missingPermission(for:)`, from the token's scopes); such an operation is not tried, spends no attempt, holds back only later operations of its own kind on its messages (an archive, trash or read change of the same message still goes, since the wait lasts until the user reconnects and an answer never moves the message), and the account reports the permission (`AccountSyncStatus.permissionsNeeded`) until a drain finds it can go. Before that, every drain drops an answer a newer answer on the same message replaces, whether it waits for the permission, sits in a backoff or may have landed, so the organizer hears the latest answer and never each one on the way; a `notFound` from an answer gives it up at once. An unsubscribe (`MailClient.unsubscribe`) stores the conversation's archive (or the end of its snooze) and its request together, and the runner sends the request after the account's other operations ("Unsubscribe" above).
6. **Pending operations win**: when a poll upserts provider state for a message that has a pending or in-flight operation, the store re-applies the pending changes on top of the provider state so an archived thread cannot pop back into the inbox before the provider catches up.
7. **Send**: `MailClient.send` stores a `PendingSend` with `sendAfter = now + undoWindow` and wakes `SendRunner`, which sends it after the window; the sent message appears via the next poll (Gmail returns `threadId`, Graph threads by `conversationId`). The runner keeps no tick: it sleeps until the earliest held send falls due, or until woken by a send being queued or by `checkForMail` (the Mac waking, the app coming to the front, the network returning), so a send whose window ended while the Mac slept goes on waking. A held send is timed on the continuous clock from the moment it is queued (or throttled), however late the runner first reads it (it may be delivering another send), so setting the Mac's clock neither cuts an undo window short nor holds a send past it, and its timer allows a tenth of a second of slack rather than the system's tenth of the wait. A send a previous run left has only its stored wall-clock time, which may have been set since, so it is held no longer than the runner ever holds one itself (60 s, a throttle's longest retry; the undo windows are shorter). With nothing held, the runner looks at the outbox only at the background poll interval (30 s), for a send stored without a wake, and the sync line's sending count observes the outbox (`MailStoring.observePendingSends`) rather than polling it, with a one-shot timer at the end of the next held send's window (the runner delivers one send at a time, so one whose window ends while another uploads is claimed, a write, only after that upload), so sending keeps no timer faster than the poll in an idle app (the other timers are under "Performance budgets"). Stopping the engine (quitting, leaving the demo) ends the runner between deliveries: a send not yet handed to the provider, one that fell due while another uploaded, stays held for the next launch rather than going to the provider under a cancelled request and failing. Claiming a send and Undo take one lock, so a message is never both cancelled and sent; Undo needs no wake (the runner finds nothing at the old time). A throttled send goes back in the outbox for the provider's retry time (the backoff schedule without one), capped at 60 s as a user action's is, and is retried then; so does one that failed before any request left this Mac (`ProviderError.neverLeftThisMac`: no route or no name for the host, no network when the task's metrics show no byte of the request left (`SentBytesProbe`; URLSession reports no network also for a request already on its way when the interface goes down, whose message may have arrived whole), or a token refresh that could not connect, as at a wake before Wi-Fi is back), on the backoff schedule, since nothing reached the provider, and at once when `checkForMail` comes first (the network back, the Mac awake); and so does one whose token could not be fetched at all (an `AuthError`: the token endpoint failing, as after a night idle with the token expired, or the Keychain failing; the HTTP clients fetch the token outside their error mapping), on the backoff schedule, or every 60 s for a refused Keychain, which asks again at each try; after any other failure it is marked failed and never retried, since the provider may have delivered it, and the app keeps it as a draft and clears its row ("Outbox" below). The runner claims one send at a time (marks it `sending`) and reads the outbox again after each delivery, so a crash or a force quit leaves in doubt ("interrupted while sending; check Sent") only the send actually handed to the provider, never one that fell due beside it.

8. **Send and Archive** (spec §5.23). What Send, ⌘↩ and Message › Send do is a preference (`AppSettings.sendAction`, `SendAction` in SSCore: Send and Archive by default, or Send), read and changed through `SendActionPreference`, which the main window points at its settings; it is not in Settings: the drop-down beside the composer's and the quick reply's Send (`SendActionMenu`, a `QuireIconButton` menu button) sets it, and a change is posted so every open composer and the quick reply retitle their Send at once (tooltip and VoiceOver name; the label stays “Send”, and a new message, which answers nothing, says Send). ⇧⌘↩ (the composer's content view and the quick reply's text view take it before the menu, as they take ⌘↩) and Message › Send and Archive always archive. Archiving happens right after `MailClient.send` has queued the message, the moment the undo window starts: the composer or the reader asks `SendArchive.archiver`, the main window (`MainWindowController.archiveConversation(answering:client:)`, only for its own engine and accounts), to archive the conversation of the message answered as E would (`MailActionRecord`, the messages in the Inbox, applied and queued through `MailClient.perform`; the return mark of a conversation back from Later kept for the undo); a snoozed conversation is archived already, so its snooze ends instead (`setSnoozes`, as E in Later). Nothing joins the app's undo history and no toast shows: the `SendArchive` it hands back travels with the send (the undo-send panel, the quick reply's `QuickSend`, and the panel a hidden bar's countdown moves to), whose Undo cancels the send and then reverts it with the inverse steps (or puts the snooze back) and reselects the conversation if the archive had taken it off the screen, exactly as Z after E; Edit › Undo reads “Undo Send and Archive” meanwhile. A send that leaves, an Undo that comes too late or a send that fails after its window leave the archive as it is. The two writes are separate transactions (the outbox's `pending_sends` row, then the archive's operation), a few microseconds apart on the main thread.

9. **Later** (spec §5.45, docs/DATA-MODEL.md "Snoozes"). L on the selection pops the Later menu (`LaterMenu`, the presets of `LaterPreset` worked out in the local calendar, then Pick a Date…, `LaterDatePicker`, a system alert sheet with a calendar and a time field), hung from the selected row or, with focus in the reader, from its header; Message › Later… and the row's context menu carry the same items. A choice calls `MailClient.setSnoozes` with the archive step for the messages in the Inbox (`MailActionRecord`, as E computes them), for the conversations a snooze can hold once archived (`Snooze.canHold`, the store's own rule: one whose mail from others is all in the Trash, as # leaves it on Microsoft with the user's reply still in Sent Items, has nothing to bring back, so L beeps for it as E does when it changes nothing, and a selection snoozes the rest): `store.setSnoozes` applies and queues the archive and writes the snoozes in one transaction, so the rows leave the Inbox and the selection moves on as after E; the toast says when they come back (“Snoozed until Tue 09:00”, or “Until …” where `ToastPresenter.fits` finds the sentence would be cut off), and its undo calls `setSnoozes` again with the times they had before (none, or the old time of a reschedule) and archive's inverse. An Archive, Move to Trash or snooze of a conversation back from Later records its `returnedAt` (`MailActionRecord.returnedAt`, read from the row), and the undo passes it with the inverse steps (`Snooze.Change.returnedAt`), so the row returns under Today with its Back tag as every undo puts a row back where it was. Only the timer lives on this Mac: `SnoozeRunner` (one per engine) returns what is due in one transaction per batch of 200 (`returnDueSnoozes`: the snooze goes, the ordinary move-to-inbox and mark-latest-unread operations are queued a millisecond apart for the mail the snooze put away (`MailActionRecord.returnTargets`), never a message in the Trash or Spam and on Microsoft not the user's own copies in Sent Items and Drafts, so the phone and the web see the same state, and the conversation is marked `returned_at`), then waits once, on the continuous clock, until the soonest snooze falls due; with nothing snoozed it keeps no timer. A due snooze first waits for its account's live lane to finish a check that began at or after its time: the lane reports each full cycle's start and, when its poll caught up, its end (`liveCheckStarted`, `liveCheckCaughtUp`, stamped on the runner's clock behind a lock so they land in order), and the runner asks the lane to check (`.check`, which leaves an account parked for sign-in parked) when it starts waiting. The outbox replays before each poll, so a return queued from what the store held when the app quit or the Mac slept would mark an old message unread on every device and tag a conversation whose overnight reply (or move to the Inbox on the phone) had not been seen yet; after the check, that mail is in the store and has ended the snooze by the rules below. The check usually lands within a second or two. An account that cannot check (offline, signed out, failing) is waited on for a minute of awake time at most (`SnoozeRunner.checkWait`, measured on `SendClock.awake`, which stops while the Mac sleeps, so a wait under way when the lid closes does not run out at wake), and then returns without it: offline, a reply that came meanwhile can still meet a return already queued. It is woken by `setSnoozes`, by `checkForMail` (the Mac waking, the app coming to the front, the network returning), by the wall clock being set (`NSSystemClockDidChange`, which a continuous wait cannot see) and by the check it waits for; it runs at launch, so a time missed while the app was closed returns then, and a time passed while the Mac slept returns at wake, each once its account has checked. No notification is posted for a return (`NewMailNotifier` skips rows with `returnedAt`). The Inbox sorts, pages and groups by `COALESCE(returned_at, latest_date_ms)` (the `conversations_inbox` index is built on it), so mail that comes back lands under Today, with a Back tag on its row while unread. The store ends a snooze on its own when its conversation is back in the Inbox (⇧I, a drop on Inbox, another device, new mail), in the Trash or Spam (everything but the user's own copies, which # leaves in Sent Items and Drafts on Microsoft and ! leaves on both; spam received after it was snoozed counts for nothing, so a reply filed in Spam never ends a follow-up on the user's own message) or gone, and when mail that is not the user's own arrives after it was snoozed; a removed account takes its snoozes. The window observes the snoozes (`MailStoring.observeSnoozes`): the rail shows the Later tile below Inbox, the title menu and the View menu list Later, and G L opens it only while something in the scope is snoozed; the Later view lists them soonest first, rows reading "Back Tue 09:00". There L moves the time, E ends the snooze and leaves the mail archived (presses within the window coalesce, `MailActionUndoHistory.coalesceExternal`), Unsubscribe sends its request and ends the snooze the same way, in one transaction, under a toast no E joins ("Unsubscribe" above), ⇧I moves it back now (the same messages its time would, `MailActionRecord.returnTargets`, also for a drop on Inbox or ⇧I on a snoozed conversation found elsewhere) and # trashes it, each undoable with the snooze restored; when the last snooze in view ends the window returns to the Inbox, keeping a search typed there (it lists All Mail, or the view an `in:` term picks, whatever the view on screen; "Search"). Later is read whole, never paged (`ShellQuery.laterLimit`), and nothing in the search grammar picks it, so a search never reads it. An undo of a step taken in Later brings Later back only when the window left it because it emptied and the user has not since picked a view or a scope or typed a search (`viewLeftAsItEmptied`, which the Outbox shares); otherwise it selects the restored rows where the user is, as every undo does.

10. **Send Later** (spec §5.46, roadmap Q2.2, docs/DATA-MODEL.md `pending_sends`). Send's drop-down (`SendActionMenu`, in the composer and the quick reply) lists, below the two send actions, the presets of `SendLaterPreset` (Tomorrow and Monday at 09:00, worked out in the local calendar as `LaterPreset` works out its own) and Pick a Time… (`SendLaterPicker`, which shows `LaterDatePicker` with Send Later's words); ⇧⌘L and Message › Send Later… hang the same items from the drop-down (the quick reply's text view takes ⇧⌘L before the menu, as it takes ⌘↩). A choice checks the message as Send does, then keeps its draft on this Mac (`ComposeDraftStore.saveNow`, before anything is queued, so a waiting message always has one) and calls `MailClient.scheduleSend(_:at:draftID:)`, which stores a `PendingSend` with status `later`, `sendAfter` the chosen time and the draft's id; nothing is archived, since nothing has gone. The composer closes (the quick reply clears) and posts `SendLaterScheduled`, which the main window turns into the toast “Sends Tue 09:00” whose Undo cancels the send and reopens the composer with the draft, or puts the quick reply's text back. `SendRunner` claims a `later` send when its time comes on the wall clock, read again at every look (the user named a time on the wall, so daylight saving and a clock set by hand move it with the wall), not timed once on the continuous clock as an undo window is, and not held to the 60 s a send an earlier run left gets; the engine wakes it when the clock is set (`NSSystemClockDidChange`). With a later send waiting the runner's wait is the poll interval's or the time's, whichever is sooner, as with any held send. A time passed while the Mac slept goes at wake. **A time missed while the app was closed is never sent at launch:** the runner's first look at the outbox, before any claim, marks every `later` send whose time has passed `missed`, which it never claims; the main window's `SendLaterOutbox` (one per window, observing the outbox) asks once, Keep in Drafts (the default) or Send Now; Send Now calls `MailClient.sendNow`, which makes them `later` and due now (still `later`, so a quit before they go asks again), and Keep in Drafts cancels them, leaving their drafts. A stop that cuts off a delivery puts a `later` send claimed in the same read back to `later`, not `scheduled`, for the same reason. A throttle at its time puts it back as `scheduled` with its draft id, timed like an undo-send's retry; the first look at launch marks such a row `missed` too once its retry time has passed, so a quit during the retry asks rather than sends at the next launch. The runner reads the outbox as summaries (`MailStoring.pendingSendSummaries`, `PendingSendSummary`: every column but the message, which `pending_sends` keeps last), reads the whole message by id only for the provider, and writes back only a send's state (`updateSend`), so a message waiting for days with a large attachment costs its looks nothing; the app's readers (the undo-send panel's and the reply field's polls, the sync line, `SendLaterOutbox`'s observation, `heldDraftIDs`) read summaries too. In Drafts, `SendLaterOutbox` hands the outbox's Send Later messages to `LocalDrafts` (the Outbox lists them too, "Outbox" below), which lists each (`SendLaterConversation`, a row kind beside the kept drafts, whose ids are kept-draft ids so every guard that keeps a kept draft away from the store keeps it away too) in place of the draft it was written in; the row and its read-only sheet are built from the outbox row (its plain-text part, set as typed), the time slot reading “Sends Mon 09:00” (`ConversationSummary.sendLater`, `SendLaterState`), “Sending…” once claimed or waiting out a throttle's retry, or “Not sent” while missed, and the sheet's date line “Sends Mon 09:00 · Cancel” (`RenderPayload.Message.cancelsSendLater`; the states are in `RenderInputs`, so a change draws the sheet again). Opening it (↩, Continue Editing) cancels the schedule (`MailClient.cancelSend`, which takes `scheduled`, `later` and `missed` sends under the claim lock) and opens its draft, and so does Continue Editing on a provider's draft that a scheduled kept draft continues (`DraftReopener`), rather than opening a fresh copy beside it; Cancel cancels and selects the draft, with Undo scheduling the draft again as it is then (`SendLaterOutbox.schedule`, which builds the message as the composer does, its attachments read off the main thread) while its time is ahead, beeping once the draft has gone (sent, discarded) or while a composer holds it; Discard takes the schedule and the draft, with Undo bringing both back the same way. Neither keeps the cancelled message for its Undo, so nothing decodes its attachments on the main thread. A row whose draft is gone (the demo's, which seeds one waiting for Monday) builds one from the message (`SendLaterOutbox.draft(for:)`). When it has gone the runner leaves the row `sent` rather than deleting it, since it carries a draft id: `SendLaterOutbox` removes the draft (and sends a provider draft it continued to the Trash, as a composer's send does), then deletes the row, so a quit between the two leaves the row to finish at the next launch rather than a stray draft of a message that went. `LocalDrafts` keeps that draft out of Drafts from the moment the outbox says `sent` until the draft store, on its own queue, has removed it, so the row never turns back into its draft. A failure keeps the draft with `SendFailureWording`'s reason, deletes the row and shows “Couldn’t send “…” · Open draft”, as for every failed send nobody else answers for ("Outbox" below). A message scheduled from the quick reply is kept as a draft too, so it opens in a composer from Drafts like any other.

## Quota budget

Each account has one `QuotaBudget`, a token bucket in the provider's own units (`ProviderQuota`). Gmail allows 6,000 units per user per minute: `messages.list` costs 5, `messages.get` 20 with metadata and 60 in full, `messages.attachments.get` 5, `history.list` 2. Outlook allows 10,000 requests per 10 minutes per mailbox, so 1,000 a minute, and Graph counts each request inside a `$batch` on its own: metadata fetched by id costs 1 request per message, a listing page with metadata inline 1, a body 2 (the message and its attachments), an attachment's bytes 1 (its `$value`). The backfill lane may spend two-thirds of the allowance, takes its units before every request and keeps at most 2 in flight; the live lane never waits for the backfill's units and records an estimate of its spend in the same bucket: each head page (plus, on Gmail, 20 units of metadata per message; Graph's carries it inline), each changes page, each body it fetches, each message the user opens and each attachment the attachment cache downloads (5 Gmail units, 1 Graph request, recorded before the request goes out; a file already cached costs nothing), since a file the user opens, saves or sees inline is as much the user's action as an opened message and never waits. A search of the server is recorded the same way, before each request: its listing (5 Gmail units for `messages.list`, 1 Graph request for `$search`) and the metadata of each hit the store lacks (20 Gmail units, 1 Graph request apiece), at most 1,005 Gmail units or 51 Graph requests per account for its 50 hits ("Server search"). A Gmail changes page is the call plus 20 units per message; a Graph one is every request the call reports it sent (`ChangesPage.requestCount`): a delta GET per folder it visits, a folder walk step's lookups and listings, and a `$batch` part per removal it looks up, three on a quiet poll, about thirteen while a full visit goes on and over a hundred on a page of removals. A Graph head page is likewise every request its call reports (`BootstrapPage.requestCount`): the system folder lookups, the folder walk's first steps and the page. The backfill's listing from the top takes one page's units before it goes out and, when the call reports more (Graph's runs the same lookups and steps), the rest after (`QuotaBudget.recordBackfill`), so its next request waits for them. The estimate leaves out the outbox and sends. The third left over is headroom for that and for the live lane's own spend (a poll every 5 s, a head page of about 2,005 Gmail units, the newest bodies), so new mail never queues behind older mail and a busy live lane slows the backfill, never the reverse. A throttle on the backfill halves its rate and pauses it until the provider's retry time, or 15 s doubling to 10 minutes (the doubling starts over once a backfill request goes through, or once ten quiet minutes have passed since the last pause ended, so throttles hours apart never add up). So does a throttle on anything that spends the live share (a head or changes page, the outbox, a body the live lane prefetches or the user opens, an attachment download, a search of the server: `QuotaBudget.liveThrottled`), since a backfill that went on spending the same per-user quota would keep the provider refusing new mail. A throttle that lands while a pause runs, from either lane, is the same event (a burst refuses the requests in flight on both): it can lengthen the pause to its own retry time but never halves the rate or doubles the pause again. The live lane never waits on the pause and keeps its own backoff; the unmetered demo pauses nothing. Every quiet minute wins back a tenth of the rate. The calendar ("Calendar") counts only where it shares the mailbox: Graph's requests for it are live spend, one request each, and a throttle on them pauses the backfill; Google Calendar is another API on a quota of its own, so its requests are recorded nowhere and its throttle holds back only the calendar. The live lane's spend also comes out of a share of its own, the third the backfill leaves (2,000 Gmail units a minute), refilling at that rate, holding a minute of it and owing a minute, or all of a single cost beyond that (so a Graph page of a thousand requests is paced by what it cost, not by a minute's worth): nothing waits on it, but the live lane reads it before what it can put off, the next page after a run or heavy changes page and prefetching the bodies a poll brought, so its own burst stays within the provider's limit as well. A head page's newest 20 bodies do not wait for it: on Gmail the head alone (2,005 units) spends the whole share, and Gmail took about 5,000 units within 12 s without refusing, so the head and 20 bodies (3,205) stay inside what it accepts. Gmail refuses a few thousand units spent in a few seconds well before a minute's 6,000 (two pages of 137 and 56 changes, 20 bodies and an opened conversation's attachments did it), so a changes page is 100 messages, about 2,000 units, the whole share. The demo's made-up mailbox is unmetered. Metered or not, the budget also holds every backfill request back while the reader waits for a body of the account (`QuotaBudget.foreground`, "Opening a message").

## Opening a message

A message opened without its body (older mail the backfill has not reached, a body Keep mail evicted, a server search's find) fetches it at once, and that body comes before everything the account does in the background, from the click to the message on screen. A mailbox serves a few requests at once per user (Graph four per mailbox, Gmail a per-user concurrency) and queues or refuses the rest, so what matters is what else is on its way when the user's request goes out.

- **The reader asks in order.** `ConversationViewController.requestMissingBodies` runs as soon as the show has read the bodies the store holds (`prepare`), before the payload is encoded and handed to the page, newest first, each with a `BodyFetchPriority`: the message the reader opens on (its target: the newest, or the one the user picked) as `.opened`; the folded rest as `.conversation`, held in the reader until the opened one has landed (each request is a task of its own, and tasks reach the engine in no set order); a pre-rendered neighbour's as `.prefetch`, raised to the shown conversation's priorities when the user moves to it. A message the user turns to while its body is still to come (a click on its card, N, P: the shell's `select`) is asked for again as `.opened` and goes at once; the engine shares the one fetch.
- **The engine sends it first.** `MailClient.ensureBody(for:priority:)` reaches the account's `Hydrator`: an `.opened` body is sent at once; lower ones wait while an opened body of the account is on its way, then go `SyncTuning.foregroundBodyConcurrency` (2) at a time, highest priority first, which with a poll and an inline image keeps a Graph mailbox within its four. From the moment a call finds no body until it returns, the budget's `ForegroundHold` is held: no backfill request goes out (`QuotaBudget.acquireBackfill` waits for it, metered or not, before its turn and again before it spends), and the live lane's prefetch of new mail's bodies and the calendar's read of its window wait for it, `backgroundYieldLimit` (10 s) at most, so an open that hangs until its timeout holds them no longer. The hold lasts 250 ms past the last reader fetch, which covers the reader asking for the folded bodies once it has drawn the opened one, and the next open a moment later. Requests already on their way when the user clicked go on: the provider has taken them, and cancelling would spend their quota again. The poll, the outbox, sends, attachment downloads and a search of the server are the user's own or new mail and never wait. An opened body stays live spend (recorded, never waited for: "Quota budget"); its throttle pauses the backfill and fails the body (the reader offers Retry); no pause or backoff of the backfill's applies to it.
- **Nothing local stands in its way.** The hydrator (one actor per account, which the backfill's and the live lane's batches share) never runs a store call itself: they run in a nonisolated hop off it, so a long scan for the bodies to fetch on a large mailbox, or a write waiting for the writer, cannot keep an open from entering. The opened body's fetch, its sanitizing and its save run on a task of user-initiated priority (the backfill's on utility), and its request goes out on a URLSession task of high priority (`ProviderRequestPriority`, which `SentBytesProbe` gives the task; the backfill's bodies low), ahead of the rest on the app's shared session.
- **Written and drawn the moment it lands.** The body is sanitized once and stored in one transaction, its mail times found (the day column reads them when it hears of the body), and the request returns; the reader updates the page at once (`ss.update`, which builds that body's frame alone) and only then tells the calendar (`onBodyArrived`). Opening never parses HTML.

Before, the reader asked for every missing body of the conversation at once, oldest first, beside the backfill's batch, so the newest body, the one on screen, waited a whole round at the provider behind its own thread and the background, and on Graph could be refused. `BodyOpenTimingTests` (`SS_PERF=1`) opens seven conversations of eight messages without bodies through the real reader and engine (debug build), on a Microsoft account whose backfill is fetching 300 more, against a provider whose server answers a body in 120 ms and serves four at once. Medians of four runs each, the code before and after taking turns so both met the same load, M4 Pro, November 2026: from the click to the body on screen 270–289 ms before, 144 ms after (the network's own 120 ms inside both); the opened body's wait at the server 121–129 ms before, none after; background requests sent while it was on its way 2 before, none after; the local shares (the budget row) 3.9–4.7 ms and 23–25 ms before, 3.6–4.1 ms and 19–20 ms after, of which sanitizing the 40 KB body is about 10 ms in a debug build. The gain is the order, not this Mac's share, which was already small. The first open of a run takes about 23 ms to send its request (the process's first render), and two background requests can leave in that time.

Two signposts follow it: `conversation.body` (category `conversation`) from the reader asking for a body it opens on, through a `stored` event when the engine has it, to the render that shows it landing; and `sync.body` (category `sync`) over the engine's share, from `ensureBody` finding no body to the body stored, carrying its priority and nothing else.

## Storage bounds

Settings › General › Keep mail on this Mac (Everything, the default; 1 year; 3 months) bounds what the store keeps whole, with the space the mailbox takes beside it. `MailEngine.setMailRetention` holds the window in `KeepMailState`, and each `AppEnvironment` keeps it on the setting (`follow`, from before the engine starts); `StorageSweeper`, one actor per engine, does the work. It keeps no timer. Work comes from three moments only: once per launch, after the first live cycle of any account, whatever its outcome (the work is local, and offline it is as useful); when a backfill ends, meaning the backfill lane goes idle after listing pages or after at least a batch (50) of paced bodies, since the few bodies of new mail the live lane hands over are not a backfill; and when the window narrows while the engine runs. Widening it, back to Everything included, evicts nothing more and fetches nothing back: those bodies return as their messages are opened. A sweep reads the window as each batch looks for candidates and again inside its write, so even the batch under way when the user widens the window evicts only to the new cutoff, and nothing for Everything, where the sweep stops.

- **Eviction.** A message received before the window keeps its row, `bodies.plain_text`, its attachment list, its design facts and its full-text row; its `html`, `text` and `rendered_html` are cleared and `bodies.evicted` is set (docs/DATA-MODEL.md). The list, snippets and search read none of the cleared columns, so they are unchanged. An evicted body keeps its row and `messages.has_body`, so `messageIDsMissingBodies` never offers it to either lane, while `body(for:)` answers nil, so opening the message fetches it at once through `MailClient.ensureBody`, the path the reader already takes for mail the backfill has not reached (the skeleton while it loads, the offline preview without a network); the save writes it whole and clears the mark. Bodies the user has in front of them stay whole until a later launch: `KeepMailState` keeps a set of them for the run, filled by `ensureBody` before it fetches and by the reader, which passes every message it renders (the conversation on screen and the neighbours it pre-renders, with a stored body or without) to `MailClient.keepBodies(of:)` before it reads any body. Every sweep of the run leaves them out, and each batch reads the set again inside its write, so a clean-up does not take the body from under a conversation on screen, a backfill ending just after the user opened old mail does not evict it again, and an old message costs one fetch per launch at most. A page can still read a body in the instant before a batch's write commits (a reader sees the last commit); the chips, Save Attachments…, Quick Look and the scheme handlers read the attachment list apart from the body (`MailStoring.attachments(of:)`), and an eviction keeps it, so they still find the files, and opening the message again fetches the body. A message shown without a body is kept too, so the backfill fetching it stores it whole and the conversation's next refresh shows it, and one that joins the open conversation without a body (an older message the backfill lists) is fetched at once, as it would be had it been there when the conversation opened. The set sits behind a lock, not on the sweeper's actor, so marking a message never waits behind a sweep step. Drafts (the drafts role), messages a live (pending, in-flight or uncertain) operation targets, and the outbox (`pending_sends`, a table of its own) are never touched. A sweep runs on the sweeper's actor in batches of 200 (`SyncTuning.evictionBatchSize`): each batch reads its candidates on a reader, so a sweep with nothing to do never takes the writer, then evicts them in one write transaction that checks every rule again, so a user action written meanwhile waits for one batch at most. In a 300,000-message mailbox a batch holds the writer about 2 ms, but about one in twenty is the commit that takes the WAL past SQLite's 1,000 pages, inside which SQLite copies the WAL into the file: 20 to 27 ms, so a key pressed in that moment during the 4 to 5 seconds of a first sweep to 1 year waits that long (`SQLiteMailStorePerformanceTests`, roadmap Q4.3). Checkpointing from a connection of the store's own instead, after every commit past the line or between the sweep's batches, was measured and gained nothing: such a checkpoint can only start the WAL over once it has caught up before the next write begins, and on APFS its sync of the file holds up the writer's own. A batch starts at the date the previous one reached (inclusive, so messages sharing that date are looked at again): `evicted` is only checked once a row has been read through `messages_by_date`, so restarting from the oldest message would walk past every body earlier batches evicted, and a first sweep of a 300,000-message mailbox would read for a quarter of an hour instead of the 4 to 5 seconds it takes. Stopping the engine stops a sweep between batches. Three other paths fetch an evicted body back, as they already did for mail the backfill had not reached: the reader's pre-render of the conversations next to the selection (through `ensureBody`, so those are kept for the run too); the live lane's prefetch of a message leaving Spam; and the newest 20 bodies of the head page a reset takes after a rejected cursor (`body(for:)` answers nil for an evicted body as for a missing one), which in a quiet account can all lie outside the window. A later sweep evicts the last two again; a reset is rare, so that costs at most 20 fetches per reset.
- **The backfill stores old mail already evicted.** A body the paced path (the backfill lane) fetches for a message received before the window is saved in the evicted form in the same transaction (`MailStoring.saveBody(_:for:evictingIfReceivedBefore:)`, under the sweep's rules), so its source and rendered HTML are never written and a large mailbox's first backfill never takes the full size on disk, which a file an earlier build created could never give back. The window and the kept set are read just before the body is saved, so a message someone asked for before then (`ensureBody` marks it before joining the fetch) is stored whole. One asked for while that save waits for the writer, or after it but before the fetch is done, joins a fetch that has already chosen the evicted form: `Hydrator.ensureBody` then finds no body in the store and saves the one it was handed whole, since the reader reads it back from the store and would otherwise stay on its skeleton with nothing left to fetch it. The live path (new mail, a message leaving Spam, a reset's head page) and `ensureBody` store whole. The sweeps remain for windows that narrow later and for mail fetched whole.
- **Attachment cache.** At most 1 GB on disk (`AttachmentCache.capacityBytes`, as Finder counts), the least recently used going first, by modification date, which the cache sets to now whenever it serves a file (access times are not kept reliably on every volume). It is measured and trimmed when the engine starts, off the main thread, and after a download once the running total passes the cap, down to nine tenths of it: a trim walks the whole folder on the cache's actor, where cached images wait, so a full cache walks it once per 100 MB of downloads, not for every download. It never deletes a file under `.partial` (a download being written), one a download in flight will read back, or the file just downloaded, and it leaves the folder a download in flight will rename its file into even when that folder's last cached file goes. Quick Look previews copies in a temporary folder of its own, so no preview holds a cache file open.
- **Maintenance** (`MailStoreMaintenance`, the SQLite store). `PRAGMA optimize` when an environment shuts down: quitting comes through `AppDelegate.prepareToQuit` to `AppEnvironment.shutdown`, which runs it after the engine's last write and before the store closes. An FTS5 optimize when a backfill ends, as 'merge' steps of 64 pages (about 256 KB), each its own transaction (`MailStoreMaintenance.mergeFullTextIndex`): the first with a negative count, which starts a merge of every b-tree, the rest positive, which carry it on even while live writes add b-trees, until a step changes fewer than two rows. FTS5's own one-statement 'optimize' would hold the writer for the whole index. The steps rewrite the whole index (about 300 steps and a second for a 300,000-message mailbox), so the sweeper takes one step per turn of its actor: stopping the engine waits for one step, not the merge, and a merge stopped halfway starts over at the next backfill's end. Incremental vacuum steps after each sweep, at the same moments: `PRAGMA incremental_vacuum(256)` (1 MiB), at most 1,024 steps per moment, only on a file whose `auto_vacuum` is INCREMENTAL. New files are created that way (docs/DATA-MODEL.md, "The file"). A file an earlier build created keeps NONE, because switching takes a full VACUUM, which the app never runs (it needs twice the disk and holds the writer throughout). Such a file stops growing while SQLite reuses the pages an eviction frees, but it does not shrink. A later release could compact it with `VACUUM INTO` a new file at a quiet launch, then swap the files.
- **The size in Settings.** The database file with its WAL and shared-memory files, less the pages SQLite keeps free inside the file for reuse (`freelist_count` × `page_size`, read on a read-only connection of its own), plus everything under the attachment cache, as allocated on disk (`MailboxFootprint`). The free pages are left out because a file from before v10 never gives space back without a full VACUUM, which the app never runs: counting them, the number would never go down after a clean-up. Measured on a background task when the General tab appears or the window reopens on it, and after the engine reports a sweep or a trim (`MailEngine.observeStorageChanges`) while the window is open. Never on a timer.

Why it matters: at about 15.6 KB a body, a mailbox of 41,000 messages reaches about 700 MB once every body is in, and one of 300,000 about 5 GB. The plain text and the full-text index are a small part of that (17 MiB of index for the 41,000-message mailbox), so a short window keeps search whole at a fraction of the size, from the first backfill on, since the backfill stores what lies outside the window already evicted.

## Search

The search field is the only search UI: no chips, no suggestions, no syntax colouring. The cheat sheet has one line listing the operators (docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md §5.32), and the empty states are the ones words already had (§5.9), whose offer to search the server ("Server search") takes operators as it takes words.

- **Parsing** (`ShellQuery`, pure, SSCore): the field's text, 120 ms after the last keystroke, becomes a `SearchQuery` (`SearchQuery(parsing:timeZone:)`, `ShellSupport/SearchGrammar.swift`): the text as typed, trimmed, and its `SearchTerm`s in order. Words; `"quoted phrases"`; `from:`, `to:` (To, Cc and Bcc), `subject:`; `is:unread`, `is:read`, `is:starred`, `has:attachment`; `before:` and `after:` with a day as YYYY-MM-DD or YYYY/MM/DD (Gregorian, local midnight in the Mac's time zone: `after:` is on or after the day, `before:` before it); `in:inbox`, `in:sent`, `in:drafts`, `in:trash`, `in:all` (the views); and a leading minus on any of them. Names and keywords ignore case, values may be quoted, terms combine with AND. Nothing is an error: an unknown operator or a value its operator does not take is a word (`label:work`, `before:yesterday`). The last term typed, while unquoted or its quote open, is a prefix (words as before, and `from:nakam`); a negated term never is, so typing `-b`, `-be`, `-ben` never leaves out every word starting with b. A last operator whose value is the start of one it takes (`is:un`, `before:2026-0`) adds nothing until finished, so the list does not flash results for the letters. `ShellQuery.make` puts the parsed search on `ConversationQuery.search` and picks the view: the first `in:` that is not negated, or All Mail; the view on screen does not apply while searching, Later included (there is no `in:later`: a search typed in Later looks through All Mail like any other), the account scope does. `SearchQuery.text` is what a provider's own search gets ("Server search"); the terms are what the stores match.
- **What matches.** Words, phrases, `from:`, `to:` and `subject:` are full-text terms: a conversation is a result when one of its messages matches every positive one and no negated one, which is how Gmail's `q` and Graph's `$search` read the same text, so a search of the server ("Server search") agrees with the local one. `is:`, `has:`, `before:`, `after:` and `in:` test the conversation's summary row, what the row shows: its unread dot (`unread_count`), star, paperclip, date (`latest_date_ms`, the latest message, never the Inbox's return time), and the view predicates (`in:` terms beyond the first narrow it, `-in:inbox` leaves the inbox out); negated, they keep the rows they do not describe.
- **SQLite** (`ConversationTable`, `FullTextIndex`): the full-text terms become one FTS5 MATCH expression, each term a quoted phrase (so FTS5 syntax typed in the field stays words) with a column filter for `from:` (`sender`), `to:` (`recipients`) and `subject:`: `(positive AND …) NOT (negated OR …)`, or, with only negated terms, the negated ones with the match inverted (FTS5's NOT needs something on its left). The query reaches its conversations by one of two plans (`ConversationTable.SearchPlan`), so its cost follows the hits or the page and never the mailbox; the plan it replaced scanned every message's key (`messages_by_conversation`) and sorted every matching conversation, which in 300,000 messages took 90 ms for a word in most of them and 123 ms for `to:owner`. *Hits first*, under 5,000 hits: each hit looked up by its rowid, its conversation kept, those conversations sorted, a few milliseconds for a rare word in any mailbox. *Newest first*, from 5,000 hits, and for a match whose terms are all negated (which describes what is outside its hits): the conversations walked newest first on `conversations_latest`, which carries every flag the view and the operators test (v21, docs/DATA-MODEL.md), each asked whether one of its messages is in the set the match produced (`+m.rowid IN (SELECT rowid FROM message_fts WHERE …)`, or NOT IN), until the page is full, so a word in most messages is answered from the first few hundred conversations. Counting the hits stops at 5,000 and costs a millisecond or two, except that the last term typed is a prefix and FTS5 merges every word that starts with a prefix before it gives the first hit, so a count would cost as much as the match and the walk would pay it again (`to:own`, nine words in 219,000 messages, 74 ms). The words are counted without the prefix mark first: their hits are among the match's, so as many as the limit settle it with no merge. A prefix of one or two letters walks. Any other prefix walks the newest 10,000 conversations and looks up the hits of the older rest only when the walk has not filled the page, so a prefix of many words merges once and one of few walks a short stretch and looks its few hits up. A search of the row operators alone walks `conversations_latest` too, since its rows sort by the latest message: the Inbox's own index would read and sort every conversation in the Inbox (40 ms for `in:inbox` in 300,000 messages, 1 ms now). What a search of the server found (`ConversationQuery.serverMatches`) joins the hits by its keys (a UNION with the hits, or a second test beside the walk's, `OR (c.account_id, c.provider_id) IN (VALUES …)`), so the operators, the view, the order and the paging apply to it alike. `SQLiteMailStoreTests.testEverySearchPlanFindsTheSameRows` holds every plan, split anywhere, to the same rows on every page. `sender` and `recipients` are full-text columns (v12, docs/DATA-MODEL.md) because the address JSON has no index that answers in 50 ms.
- **InMemoryMailStore** answers the same way (`FullTextMatcher`: the four columns, phrases and the prefix), held to it by the contract tests. Words split where unicode61 splits them, by Unicode scalar: its word characters are the letters, digits and private-use characters of SQLite's Unicode 6.1 tables, plus any code point 6.1 had not assigned, so Ⓐ and 🅻 are symbols, the Apple logo and 🥳 belong to their words, and a term with none of them (`Ⓐ`, `@`) adds nothing in both stores rather than an FTS5 phrase with no words, which would match no message. `SearchGrammar.isIndexable` asks the same question. Folding is Foundation's, which agrees with `remove_diacritics 2` for Latin letters but also drops Greek and Cyrillic accents that unicode61 keeps.
- **The open conversation.** Opening a result reads it after 500 ms (the reader's mark-read), which in an `is:unread` search would take the row out from under the reader and hand the selection to the next result, read in turn, until the list had read itself empty. So the list passes the lead, once it has rested 150 ms, as `ConversationQuery.openConversationID` while the search has an `is:unread` or `is:read` term (and only then): read state never takes that conversation out, everything else still does, and the one kept before leaves once another is kept. The list re-observes keeping its pages and selection. Apple Mail keeps the selected message in its Unread filter the same way.
- **Actions on results.** While searching, keys, menus, rail drops and the reader treat the results as rows of the view searched (`MainWindowController.listView`, the list query's view), not of the rail's: `in:trash` results restore with ⇧I and # leaves them be, as in the Trash (so the Trash folder refuses them and takes a plain search's results even with the rail on the Trash), and in a plain search (All Mail) Move to Inbox is offered whichever view is on screen. Later's rules follow the same view: L (`canSnoozeSelection`) refuses `in:trash` and `in:drafts` results and takes a plain search's typed from the Trash or Drafts, and E, ⇧I and # in a search typed in Later act as in All Mail, not as Later's own (E ending a snooze). An action toasts when it takes a row out of the list (§4.13): `MailActionCopy.wantsToast` applies the rules of the view searched and of the search's terms (archiving in an `in:inbox` search, unstarring in an `is:starred` one); read state never takes the open row out, so it never toasts.
- **Budget**: the 50 ms keystroke, with every operator alone and all of them together, on the 300,000-message fixture (`SearchOperatorPerformanceTests`, `SS_PERF=1`, release, M-series, November 2026), with Keep mail at Everything and again after a sweep to 1 year, which changes none of these: 1 ms for the row operators alone, 3 to 18 ms for names, phrases, subjects, negated words and a word in most messages (`spreadsheet`, `-invoice`, `-the`, `budget -spreadsheet`), 26 to 29 ms for every operator at once (the match is counted, then run), 6 to 19 ms with 50 conversations a search of the server found, and 45 to 47 ms for `to:owner`, a prefix whose four words are in most messages, nearly all of it FTS5's merge of them. A one-letter word prefix (`t`) is outside the budget: about 185 ms (264 ms with the plan before, 85 ms in 100,000 messages), the merge of every word that starts with the letter, which a prefix index would shorten at the cost of the index's size.

## Server search

The list's search reads only the store, so during the first download of a large mailbox it cannot find what has not arrived yet. When a search's local results have all been listed (no older page left to load), the list ends with a row that offers the server (spec §5.10): “Search Gmail for more”, “Search Outlook for more” for a Microsoft account, “Search all accounts for more” in All Inboxes with several accounts; a search with no local results offers it in its empty state (§5.9). ↩ in the search field, Edit › Search Gmail for More (no key equivalent), a click on the row's link, ↩ on the link once Tab has reached it, or the empty state's link runs it; ↩ in the field then gives the keyboard to the list, and while the field has the keyboard the row and the empty state show a “↩” keycap. The UI never calls a provider: `MessageListViewController`'s `ServerSearchController` calls `MailClient.searchServer(for:accountIDs:)` on a task and keeps the row's state; nothing waits for it and the local results stay on screen as they were.

- **The engine** (`ServerSearcher`, through `MailEngine.searchServer`) searches the accounts in scope two at a time (`SyncTuning.serverSearchConcurrency`), each with one `MailProvider.search(text, limit: 50)` (`serverSearchLimit`): the field's text as typed, trimmed (`SearchQuery.text`, operators and all), under the provider's own rules (Gmail's `q`, so its own `from:`, `is:unread` and `has:attachment` work and Spam and Trash are left out by Gmail's default; Graph's `$search` over every folder, KQL-like, as one quoted phrase with its quotes and backslashes escaped), never the local parser's terms; a '+' goes as `%2B`, since both providers read a bare one as a space. The hits the store already holds, and those the live lane wrote or deleted while a backfill runs (`backfillSkippableIDs`), need no request: the stored ones join the result as they are, and nothing about them changes. Metadata for the rest comes from `metadata(for:)` (Gmail's metadata batch of ten parts, as the backfill fetches it; Graph's `$batch` of twenty), and the hits not in Trash or Spam are written in one transaction per account through `writeBackfillMessages`, which drops in that same transaction anything stored or marked meanwhile (docs/DATA-MODEL.md, "Backfill marks"). So a hit is an ordinary row: the full-text index has its subject, people and snippet at once (a message is indexed by its snippet until its body is stored, v13), so a later search of those words finds it again with no server matches named; the backfill and the live lane treat it like any other message, and a reconcile keeps it if the provider still has it. No body is fetched: opening a found message fetches it through `MailClient.ensureBody`, the reader's path for mail the backfill has not reached, and the backfill fetches the rest of the bodies in its turn. The result names the conversations of every hit the store holds now and the accounts whose search failed (offline, throttled, signed out); a failed account never fails the others, and its error is never an alert. Cancelling the task (new words, another scope) stops the search at its next request and starts no other account; metadata already fetched is still written, since its quota is spent. Each search is a `sync.serverSearch` signpost interval.
- **Quota.** Every request is recorded as live spend before it goes out, and a throttle on one pauses the backfill (`QuotaBudget.liveThrottled`), as for any request that spends the live share ("Quota budget").
- **The list.** The found conversations join the list's query as `ConversationQuery.serverMatches`: the store lists them with the full-text hits whatever their stored text says (a body not downloaded holds the words the server matched), in date order. They stand in for the full-text terms only (words, phrases, `from:`, `to:`, `subject:`), which the server's own rules decided; everything this Mac tests on the row still narrows them: the search's `is:`, `has:`, `before:`, `after:` and `in:` terms ("Search"), the view (Trash stays out) and the account scope, so `is:unread lisbon` lists only the unread finds. A search with no full-text terms (`is:starred` alone) has no match for them to stand in for: its finds list because they are stored and fit its operators. Their order and paging are the search's (the latest message's date). A search typed in Later looks through All Mail, as any search does, and offers the server there too. The matches last as long as the words: a find the server matched only in text past its snippet lists again for later words once its body is downloaded (when it is opened, or the backfill reaches it). The list re-subscribes in place, keeping the pages it has read and its selection, as it does for the open conversation of an `is:unread` search ("Search"), so it grows rather than starting again from its first page, and reads on to the end of the finds when they take it past its pages, so the row still ends it. The row reads “Searching Gmail…” while the search runs (and until the rows it adds are read), then “Found 12 more on Gmail” (the conversations it added: found ones the list shows now and did not show before the search), “Nothing more on Gmail”, or “Couldn't search Gmail · Try again” (in a unified search the failed account by name, “2 accounts” or “your accounts”). New words, or another scope, cancel a search still running and bring the offer back with the new words' first page; an answer for an earlier search is dropped. VoiceOver hears the result once, when the list is read to its end.

## Copying an address

Every address in a message's sheet header copies with a click (docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md §5.14): each recipient of the open list, and the sender's address on the name line. The copy is the app's, never the page's.

- **By position, never by address.** The shell names what was clicked by field and position (`{kind: 'action', type: 'copyAddress', value: 'cc:2'}`, `from:0` for the sender), and `HeaderAddress` reads the address back from the message in the store, with the payload's own rule for Bcc (only on the user's own mail). The message is looked up among those the reader shows (`currentMessages`, which is `RenderPayloadBuilder.visibleMessages` of the conversation, and the replies still in the outbox), so a message the page was never given (spam, or one in the Trash beside mail that is not) names nothing. A position a re-render has taken away names nothing and is ignored. No address travels through a script message, so none can reach a log from there.
- **The app writes, then the page shows.** `ConversationViewController.copyAddress` writes `NSPasteboard.general` as plain text, then calls `ss.addressCopied(id, value, form)` for the mark and announces the copy to VoiceOver; a write the pasteboard refuses beeps, once, and shows nothing (⌘C's path beeps only for a position that names nothing). The page touches no clipboard API. A click, ↩ and Space on a focused target, ⌘C and Edit › Copy, the person menu and More › Copy Sender Address all take this one path, and nothing logs the address above debug.
- **⌘C is native.** The shell's focus report (`focusMark`) carries the focused target's position; while it does, `ReaderWebView` answers `copy:` and enables Edit › Copy, and otherwise leaves both to WebKit. The page's `copy` event is not used: cancelling it lets WebKit write its own, empty, data to the pasteboard, which can land after the app's write and erase it.
- **Keys.** The same report tells the app that ↩ and Space are the page's on a target, the recipients toggle and “and 24 more”, that ← → are on a recipient, and that Esc folds the open list (§6.1); the router lets those keys through, as it does for the elision mark and the attachment row. Only the keyboard puts focus on a target: WebKit would focus one under the pointer, unseen, so the shell cancels a press's `mousedown` there and gives focus to the sheet, as a press on a button leaves it, and the keys stay the app's after a click.

## Calendar invitations

A message that carries a calendar invitation opens with a card (docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md §5.14). SuperSparrow answers invitations and hands them to Calendar; it shows a day and a week beside the mail and writes only answers and time set aside for mail ("Calendar"). The message's body, most often the sender's own rendering of the same invitation, starts folded under the card behind the quoted-text toggle's elision mark (“Show message”, and “.”): the shell decides it from the payload's card (`RenderPayload.Message.invitation`), never from the HTML, and still loads and measures the body's frame out of sight, so opening costs what it did and unfolding goes straight to the body's height. Only the frame folds: the body's banners (remote images, Original colors) stay in view under the mark, as they stay over folded quoted text. The fold lives on the card in the shell like shown quotes, so it lasts through the store's re-renders and is forgotten by a fresh render. The app sees every key before the page, and in the reader ↩ is Open and Space is Quick Look; the shell posts `focusMark` as keyboard focus lands on an elision mark or leaves one, kept per pooled slot, and while the reader's web view has focus and a mark has the page's, both keys go on to the page, which presses the mark.

- **Read at sync time, never at open.** When a body is fetched, the hydrator stores the invitation with it (`StoredBody.invitation`, `bodies.invitation_json`, docs/DATA-MODEL.md): Graph's, which the provider read from the event message's event, or the message's text/calendar part (Gmail, the demo, and a Microsoft event message whose event Graph will not hand over), read by `CalendarInvitationReader` for the user: the account's address only. Expanding the event (`$expand=microsoft.graph.eventMessage/event`) reads the user's calendar, which Exchange refuses (403) a token holding the mail scopes alone, and an account grants `Calendars.ReadWrite` only when the user first answers; so when the event cannot be read, or reads without a UID or start, the provider fetches the message's MIME (`$value`, one more request, only for a meeting request or cancellation, never a response) and hands over its text/calendar part (`GraphMIMECalendar`: the alternative, else a calendar file; a forwarded message/rfc822 never opened). No recipient of the message is taken for the user, because none is known to be theirs: a colleague beside them (Exchange mails one request to every guest), a mailing list the invitation went to alone, the colleague whose mailbox forwards here, or whoever the user forwarded it to would be shown as “You” and answered for. On Gmail's own domains an address matches without its dots or a plus tag (j.doe+work@googlemail.com is jdoe@gmail.com), as Gmail delivers them. A group attendee is never the user. With no match the card states no answer and offers none: an invitation that does not name the account (a forward, a Bcc, an assistant's copy of the boss's invitation, an invitation to the user's other account or another of their addresses) is not this account's to answer, since the answer would speak for someone it never invited. Two cases are answered unnamed, as the account (`CalendarInvitation.answersAsAccount`): Microsoft's, where the answer goes to the event in the user's own calendar (read from the event or from the message's calendar part alike), and an invitation to a group (CUTYPE=GROUP) the user may be on, which members answer as themselves (RFC 5546); Gmail then names the address the user's own Google Calendar copy lists as them (its `self` attendee, an alias included) when the answer finds that copy. “You” among the guests is the account the message came to, never another account. Only METHOD:REQUEST and METHOD:CANCEL (or an event marked cancelled) give an invitation; a reply, a published calendar and anything the reader cannot read whole give none, and the message shows no card rather than a wrong one. Messages without one are unchanged. Opening a message reads the stored facts; the card's words (`InvitationCardText`) are formatted when the payload is built, in this Mac's zone, which is cheap and follows the Mac when it travels.
- **The reader** is ours and small: unfolding (CRLF, LF or CR; a space or tab continues a line), parameters with quoted values, TEXT escapes, components nested at most four deep, at most 512 KB of text. DTSTART with DTEND, DURATION or neither (a date lasts the day, a time is an instant); DATE (all-day, held as the day's midnight read as UTC, never shifted), DATE-TIME in UTC, floating (held as its wall clock read as UTC) or with a TZID resolved through IANA names, vendor prefixes, the Windows names Outlook and Exchange send (`CalendarTimeZones`, after CLDR's table), the VTIMEZONE's X-LIC-LOCATION, or the VTIMEZONE's own yearly STANDARD and DAYLIGHT rules (a VTIMEZONE is found by its TZID with the TEXT escapes undone, so a name with a comma or a semicolon, which Outlook gives the zones it defines, matches the quoted TZID parameter); Outlook's all-day flag on midnight times, its end from DTEND or from a DURATION counted on the wall clock, so the event keeps its days. DTSTAMP is kept, to order two revisions with the same SEQUENCE. RRULE is described in words (`RecurrenceRule`: daily, weekly with days, monthly by day or nth weekday, yearly; COUNT and UNTIL); a rule with parts it cannot say reads “Repeats”, and one it cannot read at all gives no card. ORGANIZER and ATTENDEE with CN and PARTSTAT, each address exactly one plain address (its mailto percent escapes undone; a list, brackets, quotes, a comment, mailto headers, white space or a second `@` give none, so an organizer written to reach extra recipients leaves no one to answer and no answer); rooms and resources are left out. A repeating invitation's series wins over the occurrences listed after it.
- **Answering** is an operation (`MetadataChange.answerInvitation(InvitationAnswer)`), so it follows every rule of the outbox: the answer is written on the message in the enqueue's transaction (`MessageMetadata.invitationReply`, `messages.invitation_reply`, which no provider upsert writes), the card re-renders from the store, and the engine replays it. The calendar's copy of the invitation (Google and Exchange put it there themselves) takes the answer at once as well (`CalendarStoring.answerCalendarEvents`, right after the enqueue: every occurrence for an answer to the series, the nearest one for an answer to one occurrence), so the day column and the week agree with the card, and the account's calendar lane reads the calendar once the outbox has sent the answer (`LiveLane`'s `invitationAnswered`), not before, when the provider would still say the old answer. `InvitationAnswer` carries what a provider needs without reading the store (UID, SEQUENCE, RECURRENCE-ID, times, organizer, the attendee address the user answers as, Graph's event id) and the answer it replaced, which a give-up puts back. The same answer twice queues nothing; an answer changed before the earlier one went out replaces it in the outbox (see "User action" above), carrying over the answer the earlier one replaced. A give-up puts back only an answer that went out: when an answer is given up, a newer one queued while it was on its way (which named it as the answer it replaced) is rewritten in the give-up's transaction to name what the given-up one replaced. Only the latest revision of an invitation is answered: when a later message in the conversation (the messages its reader shows) carries the same UID and RECURRENCE-ID with a higher SEQUENCE, or the same SEQUENCE and a later DTSTAMP (`CalendarInvitation.supersedes`), the earlier card offers no answer and `answerInvitation` refuses it (`StoreError.conflict`), so an answer always carries the latest revision's UID, SEQUENCE, RECURRENCE-ID and times. Microsoft's cards all read the event as it is when the body is fetched, and Graph answers the event itself, so Graph states no SEQUENCE or DTSTAMP to compare. Gmail marks the user's Google Calendar copy (`events.list?iCalUID`, `events.patch` with `sendUpdates=none`), then mails the organizer an iMIP reply (RFC 6047: METHOD:REPLY, the user's ATTENDEE with PARTSTAT, the address that copy lists as the user when there is one, the same UID and SEQUENCE, `InvitationCalendarWriter`) through `messages.send`, at once and outside the composer's undo window, since the mail alone leaves that copy at “needs action” on the phone. Microsoft answers the event (`accept`, `tentativelyAccept`, `decline`, `sendResponse: true`). The outbox retries an answer whose request may have landed (a timeout, a dropped connection, a quit mid-request), and both the mail and the Graph call reach the organizer, so each step looks first: Gmail patches the copy only when it does not state the answer and mails only when no message with the answer's Message-ID (`InvitationAnswer.replyID`, made when the user answered) is in the mailbox; Graph posts only when the event does not state the answer. An answer to an event that is gone is given up at once and the previous one put back, so the card never shows an answer nothing sent.
- **Scopes, asked for at the first answer or by Connect.** Answering needs Google's `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events` or Microsoft's `Calendars.ReadWrite` (`AccountPermission.calendar`, `OAuthEndpoints`). Sign-in asks only for mail. The providers read the token's granted scopes (`AccessTokenProvider.grantedScopes`, the scope list the token endpoint returned; unknown grants nothing); without the scope the answer waits untried, the account reports `permissionsNeeded`, the card shows its permission notice and Settings › Accounts reads “Calendar permission needed” with Reconnect. Reconnect (`MailEngine.reconnect`) asks for the permissions queued work waits for plus those the stored token already grants (and Google is asked to `include_granted_scopes`), so signing in again never drops one. It saves the new tokens through the token manager (`AccessTokenProvider.replaceTokens`), which makes the keychain write on its own actor, with nothing between it and dropping the cached token: a healthy account's cached token would otherwise stand for up to an hour without the new scope, and a refresh begun with the old refresh token, whose save runs on the same actor, lands wholly before the write (which then replaces it) or after (still answering its callers, but neither saved nor cached), never between the write and the manager's knowing, where it would put the old grant back. The reconnect wakes the account, so the next drain sends the answer and the ask goes away. The same scope shows the calendar beside the mail: Connect under the day column asks for it with nothing queued (`MailClient.connectCalendar`, through `MailEngine.reconnect(_:adding:)`), and a calendar without it never sets `permissionsNeeded` ("Calendar").
- **Open in Calendar** writes the stored calendar text (the invitation as it arrived, or for Microsoft one written from its facts at sync time: a repeating event in its own zone's wall-clock time with a VTIMEZONE written from the Mac's zone data, one observance per kind of offset change over the span the repeat can run, since a rule expands in its start's zone and Graph states a repeat's days and end date in the event's zone; the end date becomes the end of that day there, in UTC; a repeating timed event whose zone the Mac does not know, or that names none, is written as its first occurrence alone, in UTC and with no RRULE, since Graph gives its times in UTC and its repeat in that zone and the two cannot be put in one frame without it, while the card still says how it repeats) to a folder of this run's own under the temporary directory, off the main thread, and opens it with Calendar (`com.apple.iCal`), which shows the event and offers to add it. No EventKit, so no calendar permission on the Mac.
- **Cost.** One more Graph request per event message's body; nothing more for Gmail unless the calendar sits behind an attachment id (an externalized text/calendar part, or an `.ics` file sent without one, of at most 512 KB): one `attachments.get`. An answer is one Gmail `messages.list` (5 units) and `messages.send` (100 units, outside the live estimate like every send) plus Calendar API calls on that API's own quota, or two Graph requests (the event's answer, then the POST). The permission check is local.

## Calendar

Every account's main calendar shows beside the mail (docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md §5.54): a day column to the right of the reader and, when asked for, a week in the reader's place. The app reads only the store; the engine reads each calendar into it and sends the only two writes the app makes to a calendar besides answers: time set aside for a mail, and its removal. The contracts are `CalendarProvider` (beside `MailProvider`: Gmail's provider reads Google Calendar's `calendars/primary`, Graph's reads Outlook's default calendar through `/me/calendarView`, the demo's serves `DemoCalendar` and needs no Connect), `CalendarStoring` (which `MailStoring` refines: `calendar_events` and `mail_times`, docs/DATA-MODEL.md), `AccountSyncStatus.calendar` and the calendar commands of `MailClient`.

- **What is kept.** Each account's main calendar over `CalendarWindow`: two weeks back to eight weeks ahead of today, whole local days, so turning a day or a week inside it never waits on the network. The provider expands repeats into their occurrences (Google's `singleEvents=true`, Graph's `calendarView`), so nothing here reads a repeat rule. Cancelled events are left out; declined ones are kept and never drawn, so a read does not bring them back as new. An all-day event is held floating, its date's midnight read as UTC, as an invitation's is; graph's arrive with their midnights moved by the UTC preference (a Berlin holiday starts at 23:00 the evening before), so each is read back on its own dates in the zone it was made in (`originalStartTimeZone`, `GraphEventTime.allDay`). A read deletes the account's events over the window it covers that it no longer lists, and the account's history that ended more than 30 days behind the window, so the table never grows past the window. Days outside the window the app turns to are read once when it asks (`MailClient.refreshCalendars(accountID:covering:)`), and kept until a read of the window prunes them.
- **The calendar lane.** Each account whose provider adopts `CalendarProvider` has a third lane beside the live and backfill lanes (`CalendarLane`, a child of the same task group), so stopping the engine, removing the account, parking for sign-in and the launch gate reach it as they reach the backfill: it sends nothing while `LaneLink.isParked`. It keeps no timer. The live lane tells it after every full cycle that caught up, once the account is unparked and never awaiting it, and it takes a turn when its last read of the window is two minutes old while the app is frontmost or ten minutes old behind (`SyncTuning.calendarActiveInterval` and `calendarBackgroundInterval`, on the continuous clock), at once when the app asks (`MailClient.refreshCalendars`: a calendar just shown, Connect, days outside the window), when a write waits or once the outbox has sent an answer to an invitation, and after a failure once its own backoff has passed. So the first read rides each account's first live cycle, a calendar costs a read every two minutes where the poll costs twenty-four (every read lists the whole window, which is what finds the events deleted elsewhere), what this Mac does is read at once, and an outage that fails the polls asks the calendar nothing. A calendar request never runs inside the live cycle: a slow endpoint would hold back the next mail poll and the snooze runner's caught-up check, and an error thrown there would park the account, show Rate limited or pause the backfill. The window's read waits while the reader waits for a body of the account, 10 s at most; its writes do not ("Opening a message").
- **A turn.** First `hasCalendarPermission()`, which reads the token's granted scopes and sends nothing: without the scope the calendar is `.notConnected` and the turn ends, the time set aside waiting as it is. Then the account's waiting writes in the order they were made (`pendingCalendarEvents`), each read from the store as its turn comes, so a block removed meanwhile is never sent. Then one read of the window around now, every page, written with `replaceCalendarEvents` in one transaction: events inserted or replaced by their id (`CalendarEvent.localID`, the account and the provider's id), the account's events over the window that the read no longer lists deleted, the history long behind it pruned, and rows still waiting for the provider kept. The writes are looked at once more after the read, for a copy it found of a block removed meanwhile (below). Last, each interval outside the window the app asked for, read once and written the same way over that interval; a turn woken for those alone while the window's read is fresh reads only them, so turning through weeks outside the window costs a read of each, never of the window.
- **Writes.** Not operations: an operation with no message to target is confirmed without a provider call, a give-up puts back only message flags, and a dozen exhaustive switches would take a case that changes no message. Time set aside lives in `calendar_events` itself. B (or a row dropped on the calendar) calls `MailClient.addCalendarEvent`: the store inserts the block as `creating`, with a client key, in one transaction, the column draws it at once, and the engine wakes the account's calendar lane, which creates it (busy, private, no guests, no reminder, the subject as its title and a line linking back to the mail on Google, or naming the subject on Outlook) and settles the row with the provider's copy under the same local id (`settleCalendarEvent`). Remove (`removeCalendarEvent`): a `failed` row goes at once; one the provider has becomes `deleting`, never drawn, which the lane deletes there and then settles away; one still `creating` becomes a tombstone, `deleting` with no provider id under the same id and key, never drawn and never sent, since its create may have reached the provider with the reply lost. Undo (`restoreCalendarEvent`) is the inverse: a `deleting` row is `synced` again, a tombstone `creating` again under its own key (a create sent again finds any copy the first made), and a row that went comes back `creating` under a new client key. The races are the lane's to close, and every one leaves its state in the store, so a failure or a quit loses nothing. A block removed while its create was on its way settles its copy into the tombstone, and the lane deletes that copy at once, or next turn if the delete fails. A block removed after a create whose reply was lost waits for the read: it lists the copy by its key, which fills in the tombstone's provider id, and the lane deletes the copy in that same turn; a read that lists no copy sweeps the tombstone. A block whose row went altogether (its account removed meanwhile) has its copy deleted from memory. A removal undone while its delete was on its way refuses the settle with the row `synced` again; the provider no longer has the event, so the lane sets the time aside anew, a new row under a new key, and the undo holds. A create the provider refuses for good (a 4xx that is no throttle, missing scope or refused token) marks the row `failed`, which keeps its mark until the user removes it, and the turn goes on; a refused delete puts the event back as `synced`, since the provider still has it. A failure every request would meet (a throttle, the network, a refused token, the keychain, the scope, the API turned off) stops the turn there, the rest waiting and the read not taken, and the lane backs off. One that belongs to that write alone (a reply that did not read, a 5xx or a timeout for that event, the store refusing to settle it) holds back nothing: that write waits for a backoff of its own, the others and the read go on, and after `failingPageTries` (5) tries it is given up as refused, so one event the provider keeps failing never freezes the account's calendar. A create that landed but failed so is found by the turn's read under its key.
- **Idempotent creates.** A create may land and its reply be lost (a timeout, a quit, a crash), leaving the row `creating`. Its client key (`CalendarEvent.newClientKey()`, 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits) makes the next create find that copy instead of making a second: Google takes it as the event's id, so a repeat answers 409 and the copy is read back by that id, and keeps it in a private extended property; Graph takes it as `transactionId`. Every read reports the key back (`CalendarEvent.clientKey`), and `replaceCalendarEvents` merges a read event into the stored row with the same key, keeping its local id and its mail: a `creating` or `failed` row becomes `synced`, a `deleting` one stays so with the provider's id filled in. Keys are never reused, since Google keeps a deleted event's id reserved.
- **Status, never the mail's.** `AccountSyncStatus.calendar` (`CalendarSyncState`), published through `observeStatus` and written only by the calendar lane: `.unknown` until its first turn (and always for a provider without a calendar), `.notConnected` without the scope, `.apiDisabled` when Google answers that the Calendar API is off for the OAuth client (docs/OAUTH-SETUP.md, step 1), `.current(at)` after a read, and `.failing(lastRead:)` after any other failure of a read or of a failure every write would meet, the stored events still shown. It is published only when its kind changes, or for `.current` when the read is more than five minutes newer than the one shown (`calendarStatusRefresh`): the day column, its strip and the week observe the statuses and lay out again on each. The day column's foot reads it: Connect for `.notConnected`, the API's line for `.apiDisabled`, nothing otherwise. The calendar never sets the live lane's phase, message or retry time; never parks the account or drops its grant (a refused token is the live lane's to find on its own next request; the HTTP client's single 401 retry refreshes the access token, as it does for mail); and never touches `permissionsNeeded`, which the outbox rewrites on every drain and which Settings, the sync line and the invitation card read as "Calendar permission needed" with Reconnect: every account that never connected its calendar would ask for it. After a failure the lane waits a backoff of its own (`Backoff` from `backoffBase`, at most the frontmost interval), apart from the live lane's; a missing scope or a disabled API is looked at again only at the interval or when the app asks.
- **Connect.** `MailClient.connectCalendar` signs the account in again asking for the calendar scope on top of what it holds (`MailEngine.reconnect(_:adding: [.calendar])`; a plain reconnect asks only for what queued work waits for and what the token already grants), stores the new grant through the token manager as Reconnect does ("Calendar invitations"), and has that account's calendar read at once.
- **Quota.** Google Calendar is another API on a quota of its own, counted in requests rather than Gmail's units: its requests (one per 2,500 events read, one per create, plus one read after a 409, and one per delete) are charged to nothing, and its throttle backs off the calendar lane alone, never `liveThrottled`, so it never pauses the Gmail backfill. A Gmail create's link back to the mail costs nothing more: the lane hands the provider the conversation's thread id from the store (`CalendarEvent.sourceThreadID`), and the link names the account by its address (`https://mail.google.com/mail/?authuser=<address>#all/<thread>`), so a browser signed in to several Google accounts opens the right one; only a block handed over without it asks Gmail (one `messages.get` of two fields). Graph's calendar is the same mailbox under the same limit: every request that succeeds (a `calendarView` page of 999 events, a create, a delete) is recorded as live spend (`ProviderQuota.calendarRequestCost`, 1), and its throttle pauses the backfill as any live throttle does. The demo is unmetered.
- **What the UI reads.** The store and the status feed, never a provider: `observeCalendarEvents(in:)` for the days shown (declined and `deleting` rows come with it; only `isShown` is drawn), the selected conversation's invitation from its stored body as the reader's card has it, its `mailTimes(in:)` (found once, when the body was stored, by `MailTimeDetector` in `Hydrator.fetchTask`: only mail received in the last three days, not bulk mail, at most three future times with a time of day), the snoozes and the Send Later sends for its marks, and `AccountSyncStatus.calendar` for the foot. `CalendarFit` lays a day or a week out from those, with no scrolling, on the main thread. The UI calls `refreshCalendars` when a calendar comes on screen, and `refreshCalendars(accountID:covering:)` once for each range it turns to outside the window. The reader's invitation card reads `calendar_events` once per render over its invitations' time, only for a card that can say what it overlaps (`RenderPayloadBuilder.overlapCards`), and watches that span while the conversation is on screen (`followOverlaps`), rendering again in place only when a line's words change; pre-rendered neighbours are checked as they come on screen.
- **In the window.** The split keeps its three items, so its autosave, its dividers and their tests are what they were: the reader's item is a container (`ReaderAndCalendarViewController`) holding the conversation pane, the day column at its trailing edge, the column's 28 pt strip and the week, laid out by hand in one frame on every resize, never animated. The column has one width and no divider; whether it is on is the app's setting (`AppSettings.showsDayColumn`, on by default), not the split's autosave, so a new window opens as the last was left and tests keep theirs apart. Below 640 pt for the container (a 400 pt reader beside 240) the column folds to its strip; a click on it or D opens the column over the reader's edge (a cover over the reader takes the click that closes it), and widening the window puts it back in place. While the column is on the reader's item keeps 428 pt and the list's maximum gives up 28, so even the strip never takes the reader under 400. The week takes the container's whole width with the conversation pane hidden, never removed, so the pool's web views and their pre-renders stay attached and the reader keeps following the selection underneath. One `CalendarState` per window holds what the views draw; `MainWindowController+Calendar` is its shell: it sets the state's inputs (the shown day and week, the span, the scope from ⌘0…⌘9, the accounts, the selection) and answers `CalendarViewDelegate`.
- **Keys and menus.** D, W, [, ], T and B are `KeyCommand`s in the router's table, so they never fire in a field and an armed G keeps its own (G D, G T); D and W ignore their key repeat, [ ] and T act only while the column or the week shows. View › Hide Day, Show Week, Previous Day, Next Day and Today (retitled for the week) and Message › Block Time carry the same keys and validate the same way. The calendar's toggle is never in the reader header: once the engine says some account has granted the calendar permission (`CalendarState.calendarAccess`, remembered in `AppSettings.hasCalendarAccess` for the next launch's first frame) it stands at the trailing edge, in the column's header, or atop the strip a hidden column then folds to (`ReaderAndCalendarViewController.hasCalendarAccess`), whose click shows the column in place when the window has room. Reader windows pass D, W, [, ] and T to the page and take B for their own conversation, with its toast there. Esc follows §6.1: an event's details first, then the toast, the find bar and the recipients, then the column over the reader's edge, then the week's invitation card, then focus in the reader or the calendar back to the list. The calendar's stops come after the reader's in the window's Tab loop (`MainWindow.calendarKeyViews`).
- **Following the mail.** `present(selection:)` hands the selection to the state, which reads its context off the main thread; when it lands the window turns the column (or the week) to the invitation's day or the first time ahead its mail names, else back to the day the user last chose ([ ], T, a day cell, a heading), and turns it once: it is the state's first observer, so the views refit for the new day only. A body the reader fetches reads the context again (`ConversationViewController.onBodyArrived`), since the times are found as it is stored. W opens on the column's day's week; turning the week never moves the day the column comes back to.
- **Time set aside, in the window.** B, a dropped row and a click on the mail's time build the block (`CalendarBlock`: 30 minutes, the subject or “No subject”, the conversation's newest message and account, a new client key) at the first time ahead the mail names, else at the first free half hour from the day shown, and call `addCalendarEvent`; a selected invitation gets the notice that points to its card instead. Each block and each Remove is one external step of the undo history (`recordExternalUndo`: “Undo Block Time”, “Undo Remove Block”), its toast saying when, its revert `removeCalendarEvent` or `restoreCalendarEvent`, so Z and ⌘Z reach it as they reach an archive. Join and Open in Google Calendar or Outlook open only https web addresses, and only those are offered (the calendar readers keep no other link, and a pencil takes its invitation's meeting link only when it is https), so no button does nothing; and the foot's How to turn it on the setup guide the app bundles (or Google's page for the API); Show the mail selects the conversation, moving the list to a view that holds it. Connect asks each account lacking the calendar in turn through the app delegate's sign-in, the one Reconnect uses, and stops at a refusal or a cancel. The demo adds a Later sample once its first mail is in (`DemoSeeder.seedLater`), so the Later mark shows from the start.
- **The minute tick.** The engine keeps no calendar timer. The now line is the UI's: it moves once a minute (`dur.nowTick`) only while a calendar shows in a window on screen and not occluded, and the shown day turns at midnight on `NSCalendarDayChanged`.

## Rules

Sender rules (Always Archive Mail from This Sender, Block Sender; spec §5.52) live on the server: a Gmail filter, an Outlook inbox rule, which the provider runs as mail arrives whether or not this Mac is on. The server is the single source of truth: there is no rule engine on this Mac, the app shows the server's rules and changes them on the server. The store keeps `sender_rules` as a mirror of the server's sender rules (for an instant, offline display, and for the snooze rule) plus the user's changes on their way there (docs/DATA-MODEL.md "Sender rules"), so a rule set or removed is shown at once and never blocks the UI on the network. The contracts are `RuleProvider` (beside `MailProvider` and `CalendarProvider`), the store's sender-rule calls (`setSenderRule`, `replaceServerSenderRules`, `settleSenderRule`, `pendingSenderRules`, `observeSenderRules`), `AccountSyncStatus.rules` (`RulesSyncState`) and `MailClient.refreshServerRules` / `connectRules`.

- **What a sender rule is on each server.** Recognition is exact, and anything else is one of the server's "other" rules, counted (`ServerRuleListing.otherCount`) and never changed. Gmail (`users.settings.filters`, `GmailFilters.swift`): criteria of `from` alone holding one address (`ServerSenderRule.singleAddress`: no spaces, `OR`, braces, quotes, wildcards, negation; Chat's `excludeChats` allowed), and an action that removes `INBOX` and adds nothing (archive) or adds `TRASH` and nothing else, removing at most `INBOX` (block), never a forward. Created exactly so (`{"criteria":{"from":addr},"action":{"removeLabelIds":["INBOX"]}}`, `…{"addLabelIds":["TRASH"]}}`). Gmail runs every matching filter, so an archive filter beside a trash filter for one address is the block, both ids kept so removing it deletes both. Graph (`/me/mailFolders/inbox/messageRules`, `GraphProvider+Rules.swift`): an enabled rule whose conditions are `fromAddresses` alone with one address, with no exception, and whose actions are a move to the Archive folder or a delete that is not permanent, each with at most `stopProcessingRules`. The Archive folder is known by its id in both of Graph's forms (`GraphProvider.ArchiveFolderIDs`), since a rule's `moveToFolder` may name it in the default REST form while every other request asks for immutable ids: the immutable id from the well-known folder cache (looked up once when the cache lacks it), and the default form from one `GET /me/mailFolders/archive?$select=id` sent without the immutable-id preference (`GraphRequest.immutableIDs`), kept for the provider's life (`GraphFolderCache`, looked up again only when the immutable id changes); a move to either is an archive, never guessed: without the folder, a move is another rule and an archive cannot be created. Created last in the order (`sequence` after the highest, which the create reads the rules for), enabled, stopping the rules after it, moving to the Archive's default-form id, named "Archive mail from <addr>" or "Block <addr>". Disabled rules are other rules. Both providers create a rule only for an address `ServerSenderRule.singleAddress` accepts, and refuse any other before a request (`RuleProviderError.refused`): Gmail reads `from` in its search grammar, where a leading `-` would negate the sender (a block taking everyone else's mail), braces would group and a bare name match far more, and a rule the read would not show again would vanish from Senders while the server kept running it. Only the sender's address and the action ever reach the provider; addresses are never logged above debug, nor a refusal's message (only Graph's one-word error code).
- **The rules lane** (`RulesLane`, one actor per account whose provider adopts `RuleProvider`, beside the calendar lane, a fourth child of the account's task group). A turn: nothing while the account is parked (`LaneLink.isParked`); then the permission, read from the token's granted scopes (no request): without `AccountPermission.rules` the status is `.notConnected` and nothing is sent; then the rules nobody wants are deleted (below), before the read could find them; then one read of the server's rules into the store (`replaceServerSenderRules`), which is also the read before every create; then the rows waiting, oldest first (`pendingSenderRules`). A `creating` or `replacing` row is made the one rule the server holds for its sender: an identical rule the read found is adopted (a create whose reply was lost, a rule made on another device), otherwise one is created and recorded at once (`settleSenderRule(.written)` before anything is deleted, so a removal meanwhile or a failed delete never leaves a rule on the server the store does not know), then the row's old rules and any rule for the address with the other action are deleted and the row settled `saved`. A `deleting` row's rules are deleted (one the server no longer has counts as deleted) and the row goes. A rule made for a row that went while its request was on its way (removed before it reached the server) is one nobody wants: deleted at once, or first thing next turn, and never written into the store by a read meanwhile (a rule set again for the sender is created anew, never adopted onto it). So is the rule a create whose reply was lost (the network or the service failing after the request may have left) made for a row the user removed before the next read: that read finds it with no row for its address and deletes it, rather than show it as the server's rule and undo the removal. A refused delete or replacement is put back as the server holds the address's rules at that moment (a replacement's new rule made before the old one's delete was refused included), folded as a read folds them. Then `AccountSyncStatus.rules` says `.upToDate(otherCount:readAt:)`.
- **When.** It keeps no timer, as the calendar lane keeps none: at launch after the live lane's first cycle that catches up; at once when a write waits (`MailClient.setSenderRule` wakes it) or when the app asks (`refreshServerRules`, for Settings › Senders opening; `connectRules` after the reconnect); otherwise when the last read is 30 minutes old while the app is frontmost and 2 hours otherwise (`SyncTuning.rulesActiveInterval`, `rulesBackgroundInterval`), looked at whenever a live cycle catches up; after a failure, once its own backoff has passed. While the scope is missing every live cycle looks again (no request), so a reconnect from any path that granted it sends what waited. A read nobody asked for yields to the reader's body fetches, as the calendar's does. A slow rules request never holds back a mail poll.
- **Failures.** `RuleProviderError.permissionMissing` (Google's 403 `insufficientPermissions`; Graph's 403 on the rules while the token lacks the scope) is `.notConnected`: the writes wait, and `MailEngine.permissionsToAsk` adds `.rules` to every reconnect while a sender rule waits for the server (the scope is also kept once granted, as every permission is). `.limitReached` (Gmail's cap on filters, Exchange's rules quota) and `.refused` (any other permanent 4xx; on Graph also a 403 the token holds the scope for, an organization's refusal that a reconnect would not fix) give a create up (the row goes) and put a delete or a replacement back as the server has it; the status says `.failed(.limitReached | .refused, otherCount:)`, with words for the app (`RulesSyncFailure.words(for:)`), through the reads that follow until a later write goes through. Everything else (the network, the service, a throttle, the token) backs the lane off and says `.failed(.unreachable, otherCount:)` until the next read; a write that keeps failing for a reason of its own (a 5xx for that rule) is given up as refused after `SyncTuning.failingPageTries`, so it never holds the others back, while a failure every request would meet costs a rule none of its tries. Nothing reaches the mail: no phase, message or retry time, no `permissionsNeeded`, no park.
- **Quota.** Both providers' rules spend the mail's quota, charged as live spend (`ProviderQuota.ruleReadCost`, `ruleCreateCost`, `ruleDeleteCost`): Gmail's settings calls at its per-method units (filters.list 1, create and delete 5), Graph's as mailbox requests (a page of rules or a delete 1, a create 2, since it reads the rules for its order; a read's folder lookups counted with it). A throttle on them pauses the backfill as any live throttle does.
- **Arrival.** The store applies no sender rule at arrival (docs/DATA-MODEL.md "Arrival rules"): the server archived or trashed the mail before this Mac saw it. The mirror is read only so that mail from a sender with a rule never ends a snooze (`ArrivalRule.keepsSnooze`). Setting a rule still applies its change, once, to that sender's mail in the conversation it was set from (`SenderRule.Action.change(for:)`, the steps `setSenderRule` performs), since a server rule acts only on mail that arrives after it exists.
- **The demo.** `DemoMailProvider` adopts `RuleProvider` over its mailbox, in memory (the permission granted, the blocked sender from the start and two other rules to count); `DemoSeeder` writes the blocked sender as the server's rule (`saved`), and a later launch's server starts from the rules the store mirrors (`DemoMailService.serverRules(mirroredIn:accountID:)`).

## Rendering

- Sanitize at sync time with SwiftSoup: strip `script`, `iframe`, `object`, `embed`, `form`, `input`, `meta`, `link`, `base`, event-handler attributes, `javascript:` URLs; keep `style` attributes and `<style>` blocks (the body is isolated in a sandboxed iframe); rewrite `cid:` images to `ss-cid://` URLs; replace remote image `src` with `data-ss-src` and remote `background=` with `data-ss-background`, and count them; strip `url(http…)` from inline CSS (counted, but not restorable by Load images); wrap detected quoted content in `<div class="ss-quote">`.
- The conversation view is one `WKWebView` showing a shell document (CSS and JS loaded once). Rendering a conversation is one `evaluateJavaScript("ss.render(json)")` that builds cards and places each body in `<iframe sandbox srcdoc>`. Inline images load through a `WKURLSchemeHandler` for `ss-cid` that reads attachment bytes from the local cache (fetching through `MailClient.attachmentData` when missing).
- An attachment chip's download (spec §5.17) shows once the fetch outlasts 150 ms, so a cached file never flashes it. `MailClient.attachmentData` takes the listed size and a progress handler: a provider that streams reports fractions as the bytes arrive, the attachment cache hands them to every caller of that download at most about ten times a second (`AttachmentProgressRelay`: the first and the last at once, one held fraction delivered when the interval is up), and the reader hops them to the main thread, never through the store, where `AttachmentDownloadBoard` turns them into the chip's contrail: determinate at the latest fraction, sweeping only while the size is unknown (no listed size and no Content-Length).
- Download (spec §5.17), the visible button on every chip, is the chip menu's Save to Downloads: the same fetch through `fetchAttachmentFile` (the chip shows it as above), then `AttachmentSaver.copy` into Downloads under a free name (“plan 2.pdf”), the quarantine kept, and the saved toast. Download all, on a message with two files or more, and File › Download Attachments (⇧⌘D, the target message) fetch the message's files one after another through the same session files and show one progress on the message's count line, not on the chips: each file's fraction weighted by its listed size (`AttachmentDownloadBoard.batchShares`), one `AttachmentDownloadBoard` entry keyed by `Chip.allAttachments(of:)`, `ss.attachmentsProgress` in the shell. A file that cannot be fetched marks its chip and the rest are saved. `AttachmentSaveOutcome` (SSCore) words the toast the main window shows, a notice with Show in Finder (`ConversationAction.attachmentsSaved`). A second press while a save runs does nothing, nor one on the same control within a double-click interval of its end (a file already on this Mac is saved before the second click lands; the shell also drops a click whose `detail` is above 1); a chip's Download does nothing while its message's Download all runs, and Download all leaves a file whose own Download runs to it. Progress, its end and a failed fetch go to the pool slot whose page holds the message (`renderedInputs`), on screen or not, since coming back to a warm slot is a swap with no render. The shell posts `downloadAttachment` and `downloadAllAttachments` actions and reports a focused Download control as it reports an elision mark, so Space and ↩ press it. Nothing here goes through the store or the outbox: saving a file changes no mail.
- Dragging attachments (spec §5.17, Dragging out; §5.18; §5.20). The page never drags: chips and Download all are `-webkit-user-drag: none`, and a primary `mousedown` on one posts `attachmentPress` (the message, each file's id and chip rect, the press point, page coordinates). `ReaderWebView` keeps the native press (`mouseDown`) and arms it with that message only while the button is down and only if the message's point is the press's (a late message, or one about another place, is dropped). The first `mouseDragged` past `dragCard.threshold` (3 pt) is not passed to WebKit: `AttachmentDragController` builds one `NSDraggingItem` per file and starts `beginDraggingSession` from the web view with that very event, the copy operation only (nothing leaves a message). Before handing WebKit a mouse-up at the press point (as WebKit does after a drag of its own, since the session swallows the real one), the reader tells the shell (`ss.attachmentDrag`), which dims the chips and swallows the click that mouse-up makes; the session's end undims them. Each item is an `AttachmentFilePromise`: an `NSFilePromiseProvider` that is its own delegate (the provider's delegate is weak; the drag pasteboard keeps the provider until the next drag), of the file's `UTType` (from the MIME type, else the extension, always data, since a promise must be) under the name `AttachmentSafety.safeFilename` makes, which also writes the app's own `io.supersparrow.mac.attachment` type: a JSON `MailAttachmentReference` (the message id and the stored `AttachmentInfo`). The image is an `AttachmentDragCard`, drawn once into an sRGB bitmap at the window's scale and in its appearance, its `e2` shadow in a margin of its own, the card starting exactly over its chip. Blurring `e2` was most of a card's cost, so the shadow is blurred once per theme, mode and scale under a card just wide enough for both rounded ends, and drawn under each card in three parts, the middle column stretched (every card is the same height, and its shadow the same along its length away from the corners).
- Keeping a promise. The Finder (the Desktop, a folder, any app that takes files) asks for the file once the drop lands; AppKit calls the provider on the queue it names, a private `OperationQueue`, never the main one. `AttachmentPromiseFulfiller` fetches the bytes through `MailClient.attachmentData` (the attachment cache: a cached file costs a disk read and nothing on the quota, a miss downloads through the provider as Download does and is live spend; the UI never calls a provider) and `AttachmentSafety.deliver` writes them under a hidden partial name beside the destination, marks it quarantined and reads the mark back, then renames it into place with `renamex_np(…, RENAME_EXCL)`, so the file never carries its name unmarked and two writes can never land on one name; a name already there gets the Finder's numbering instead of being replaced, and a volume that keeps no extended attributes fails the write with nothing left. Promises are kept one at a time, first come first served (`AttachmentPromiseLine`, one for the app), as Download all saves: a drop of twenty files holds one file's bytes in memory and asks the provider for one at a time. The names one drag promises are unique within it (`PromisedNames`: “image.png”, “image 2.png”, compared without case), since a receiver other than the Finder reads each file at the URL it handed out. Its events reach the reader on the main queue in the order they happened: the chip shows the download after 150 ms (`AttachmentDownloadBoard`, as for Download), a fetch that fails marks it, and either failure posts `ConversationAction.attachmentDragFailed` for the window's error toast (`AttachmentDragFailure`'s words). A promise outlives its reader: when no reader takes its end (a reader window closed meanwhile), the failure goes to `AttachmentDragController.onOrphanedFailure`, which a reader window points at the main window, and a reader window whose controller has gone forwards `attachmentDragFailed` there as it forwards `attachmentsSaved`. The completion handler ends the promise with the error, so the Finder shows nothing; nothing shows at the destination until the whole file has its name, since only a complete, marked file ever takes it. Errors are logged by domain and code; a file name only at debug.
- Dropping attachments in the app. `AttachmentPasteboard.dropItems(for:)` reads a drag: the app's references only when its source is an `AttachmentDragController` (a drag from another process names no source), else file URLs; any process can write the reference type, so `ComposeWindowController.attachDropped` also resolves every reference, dropped or pasted, against `client.store.attachments(of:)` and takes the store's `AttachmentInfo` (name, type, size), dropping one no stored message lists with the `droppedAttachmentsGone` status. `ComposeContentView` registers both types (the composer's text view and the strip register neither the reference nor the promise types, so the drag reaches it from anywhere in the window; the field editor of To, Cc, Bcc and Subject does register them, so the window returns its own `ComposeFieldEditor`, which hands files and references to the content view) and shows its drop outline only for them, and not while the message is being handed over; `ComposeWindowController.attachDropped` attaches files through `attach(_:)` as before and each reference as a `ComposeAttachment.loading` chip whose bytes `client.attachmentData` fetches, Ready as `.data` or, failing, removed with the `droppedAttachmentFailed` status; Send and Send Later wait for those fetches (the button shows it is sending and the fields hold still, but `isSending` stays false: the window closes keeping the draft, Esc stops the wait, removing a loading chip cancels its fetch and lets the send go) and send nothing if one failed meanwhile. `AttachmentDropOverlay` keeps its words clear of the card under the pointer (`AttachmentDragController.Drag.card`, the card relative to the pointer), beside it or above or below it, shortening the quick reply's to “Drop to attach” where the full words fit nowhere clear. `QuickReplyView` registers both types too (its text view, which takes file URLs, hands files to it, and its glyph image views are unregistered, since an `NSImageView` registers for files and promises even when it cannot be edited), and takes files only when one is a readable file, never a folder; a drop calls `openQuickReplyInComposer(…, dropped:)`, so `ConversationAction.replyInComposer` carries the items to `ComposeWindowController.present(…, dropped:)`, which attaches them as a drop on that window would. The list, the rail and the calendar register only the conversation type, so AppKit never offers them these drags, and `ReaderWebView` refuses any drag that carries the app's reference.
- Keep a pool of three warm web views. When the selection lands on a row, render the next row, the previous row, and the next unread conversation into spare views. Selection highlight moves immediately; rendering is debounced by 30 ms while a key repeats, and pre-rendering waits until the selection has rested for 150 ms.
- Every `WebViewPool`'s configuration, a reader window's too, is a copy of one template, with its own user content controller, preferences and scheme handlers, so all of them are on the app's one WebKit process pool while each view keeps a WebContent process of its own. WebKit reads the fonts the user installed for a pool's new processes once (every font's properties from the font server, over a synchronous call, on the main thread: about 85 % of a view's first load, which took 13 to 21 ms on an M4 Pro with 73 fonts installed, and takes longer with more); a configuration made afresh has a pool of its own and pays that read again, which every reader window did inside its ⌘O.
- A read or star change in the shown conversation patches the card headers (`ss.flags`) instead of re-sending bodies. Whether a body has quoted text and how many remote images it parks are stored with it at sync time, so no render scans HTML.
- Dark mode: the shell adapts; email bodies get `color-scheme: light dark`. Styled mail that declares its own colours is adapted to the theme's inks, or shown as sent on a white page, by Settings › Appearance › Dark Mode (spec §5.14), and each message keeps a per-session switch (⇧⌘O). Every body frame sits in a permanent page wrapper whose class is the white page, so the switch, the default and an appearance change toggle classes and refit, and never build a frame again. AppKit tells no view outside a window of a new appearance, and WebKit turns `prefers-color-scheme` only when AppKit tells the view, so the pool gives its views outside the window (the pre-rendered neighbours) the window's appearance, following the app's and the shown view's, and they restyle out of sight instead of on their first frame on screen. WebKit may turn the query before it delivers the media `change` event, so the shell also hears of the appearance from its view, a theme switch and a mode change, and acts once per change.

## Print

File › Print… (⌘P, spec §5.48) prints the open conversation through the system print panel, whose PDF menu saves it or opens it in Preview. It prints the conversation's sheets, not the screen. `ConversationViewController.printConversation` asks the reader's shell what each card shows (`window.ss.cardStates()`: images loaded, quoted text and the message under an invitation card unfolded, the live remote sources it holds back; read into `PrintSheetState`, and a message the shell cannot speak for prints as a fresh render shows it), builds the payload again from the store as a render does (so every message has its body, whether or not its card was ever opened), and `PrintDocumentBuilder` (SSCore, pure) turns the two into one HTML document: the subject with the participants and the count, then every message, oldest first, with its header written out for paper (the address beside the name, every recipient, the date in full in this Mac's zone), its invitation card without buttons (the answer given written in its list), its body, a note for what the reader folds or holds back, and its attachments as lines. Nothing parses HTML: the stored bodies go in exactly as the reader got them.

- **One page flow.** A body is not a frame, which a printer cuts through mid-line, but a shadow root in the document, so it paginates like the rest of the page (a break falls between lines, an image is never split) while its styles stay its own; each gets an `html` and a `body` element of its own, so a sender's rules for them apply as in the reader's frame, and the reader's body CSS (`PrintDocumentAssets.bodyCSS`) sets plain and unstyled bodies on the reading page and folds quoted text by the same classes. The sanitizer removes `template`, `slot` and `script` elements and escapes every text node, so a body cannot close the `<template>` it waits in. Each body's host is what its frame is in the reader, its own containing block, formatting context and paint scope (`position: relative`, `display: flow-root`, `isolation: isolate`), clipped top and bottom (`overflow-y: clip`; left and right stay open, so a wide newsletter still shrinks to fit): what a sender positions absolutely, floats, lifts with a z-index or pulls up with a negative margin stays in its own sheet and never covers the masthead or another message's header, so a printout cannot be made to say someone else wrote it. What a body places beyond the end of its own flow is cut there, where the reader's frame would grow to it.
- **No body's script runs.** The reader's frames are sandboxed and run none; on paper every body is in one page with JavaScript on and the `ready` handler in reach, so the page's first element after the charset is its own policy, `script-src 'nonce-…'; object-src 'none'` (`PrintDocumentAssets.scriptPolicy`), with a nonce of 128 random bits made for each document (`PrintDocumentBuilder.makeNonce`) on the document's one `<script>`. An event handler, a `javascript:` URL or a script element that got past the sanitizer is refused from the first byte; the policy says nothing about images, styles or fonts, which stay the reader's policies' to decide.
- **Images as the reader shows them.** One document has one Content-Security-Policy, and the reader gives each body its own, so the document's script applies the reader's in two steps (`PrintDocumentAssets.script`). The bodies wait inert in `<template>`s. Those whose images the reader shows are attached first, their parked sources put back as Load images does, and laid out, so everything they fetch is asked for; then, if any body's images are held back, the reader's blocking policy (the shell's `BLOCKING_CSP`, `img-src ss-cid: data:` and nothing else remote; a test holds the two strings equal) is added to the page, which WebKit enforces on every fetch asked for after it, and only then are those bodies attached. Printing therefore fetches nothing the reader held back, a body stored while Load remote images was on (whose sources are live) included. A held-back image takes no room on paper, where its one-pixel stand-in would only push the page on, and a note says how many were left out. Inline images load through the reader's own `ss-cid` scheme handler, in held-back bodies too. Each step waits for its own images, by their load and error events (not a timer poll, which WebKit slows to once a second in a page that is never on screen), up to `motion.duration.printImageWait` from when that step began: a slow tracking pixel in a body shown first never leaves a held-back reply's inline screenshot empty.
- **Folds.** Quoted text prints as the reader shows it: folded, it is left out and a note marked with the elision glyph (“Quoted text hidden”) stands where the mark does; unfolded, it prints. The message under an invitation card likewise (“Message hidden”). To print what is folded, unfold it first.
- **Printing.** `ConversationPrinter` loads the document into a web view of its own, made for that print and let go when it ends: Aqua (WebKit lays a page out for print in a light appearance anyway, so the paper is light whatever the app shows), JavaScript on for the document's own script (the only one its policy lets run), backgrounds printed, incremental rendering left on (suppressed, WebKit paints nothing until the page's load ends, which an image that never comes holds off, and the page would print blank), navigation to nothing but the document. Once the script posts `ready` (each step's images in, or its `motion.duration.printImageWait` gone by, after which an image still on its way prints empty; a page that never says so prints two seconds after both waits), it runs `WKWebView.printOperation(with:)` as a sheet on the main window (`runModal(for:)`, the one way WebKit's printing view lays out its pages; run synchronously it keeps asking for pages it has not measured). The margins are `layout.print.pageMargin`; WebKit lays the page out 1.25 times as wide as the printable width and scales it to fit, as Safari prints. The job's name, which Save as PDF offers for the file, is the subject. The paper chosen in the panel is kept for the next print while the app runs. One print at a time: Print… is disabled while a printout is prepared or its panel is up, and with anything but one conversation open. Tests and the print renders send it to a PDF file with no panel (`ConversationPrinter.Destination.file`).
- **Cost.** Nothing until ⌘P. A print is one payload build (the conversation's bodies read once) and one page load in a web view that lives for that print; the images the reader shows come from the cache, inline ones from this Mac.

## Find in conversation

⌥⌘F (Edit › Find in Conversation…) opens a find bar (`QuireFindBar`, spec §5.50) floating over the top of the reader, so the web view's frame never changes. The search runs in the shell, which owns the documents; nothing touches the store, and nothing is queued, since finding changes no mail.

- `ReaderFind` (the app) sends the shell `ss.find(query)`, `ss.findStep(±1)` and `ss.findClear()`, one call in flight at a time, a newer query replacing any not yet sent. The shell posts `{kind: 'find', query, count, index}` after each, and after a store refresh (`ss.update`) or a render of the same conversation finds again in place; the bar shows only the report for its own query from the slot on screen.
- What counts: every message's body, collapsed and folded ones and quoted text included; not headers, the invitation card or attachment names. Comparison folds case, accents and white space, and a match never crosses a block. A framed body is walked in its frame; a body with no frame is read from its payload HTML through `DOMParser` (no browsing context, so no script runs and nothing loads) by the same walk, so the count is the same whatever is expanded. Text hidden by the element's own style counts for nothing, read from `element.style` as the browser parsed it: `display: none`, `hidden`, a zero opacity or max-height hide everything inside, while a zero font size or `visibility: hidden` pass down only until an element sets a size of its own or `visibility: visible` (MJML's columns). Text hidden by a sender's stylesheet rule cannot be told from the HTML and still counts. A parsed body keeps only its folded text, its plain-ASCII text nodes folded whole, so the first find in a conversation, which reads every collapsed body, stays inside the keystroke budget.
- Matches are `mark.ss-find` elements wrapped around the text pieces in loaded frames (a frame that loads later is marked then); `ss.findClear()` unwraps them and joins the split text nodes, leaving the body as parsed. Frames keep `sandbox="allow-same-origin"` and the blocking Content-Security-Policy; the shell adds no script, source or attribute to a body beyond the marks.
- Folded content: a step opens only what hides the current match (the collapsed message, its own folded run, the message under an invitation card, its quoted text), without animation, and makes that message the target; it stays open after the find ends. Typing opens nothing until it rests for 300 ms (`FIND_REST_MS` in the shell), and then only for the match current by then, so the prefixes of a word never open the messages they matched; a match in sight is scrolled to at once. Other matches are counted, never opened.
- The bar closes when the reader shows another conversation, several or none, or the conversation empties. Opened while the selected conversation's render is still landing in another slot, `ReaderFind` holds its calls until that slot is on screen, and a slot it stops searching gets `ss.findClear()`, so the page being left is never searched or left marked. The query is kept for the session in memory only (never the system's find pasteboard), in one `FindSession` the main window hands its reader and every reader window it opens, so ⌥⌘F and ⌘G find it again in the next conversation, in whichever window.

beside them the hand-written runtime: `QuireFont` (cached fonts, exact line heights, the New York coverage test, and the Font setting: under SF Pro every New York role resolves to its sans twin before a font is looked up, so caches key on the role actually set), `QuireEnvironment` (Graphite, Increase Contrast, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, and the Font, Text size and theme settings the app pushes into it at launch and on every change, as a thread-safe snapshot plus a change notification; the typeface is part of `cacheKey(for:)`, which stays theme-free, so no sender's avatar face is drawn again for a theme; the list adds the Text size and the theme to its rows' key, and the theme to its account faces' key, and every view that holds a font or a laid-out string re-applies on the notification, so a switch is live everywhere, the reader's pooled web views included, without a relaunch; a Font switch re-measures nothing), Text size (spec §4.15: `QuireFont.TextSize`, a step every role takes in size and line height, held at 11 pt; `QuireFont` takes the size as a parameter, Default unless asked, so only the surfaces where mail is read and written ask for `QuireEnvironment.textSize`: the list's rows, heads and skeleton through `QuireRowMetrics`, which steps a row 64 / 68 / 72 / 76 pt (compact 44 to 56) and re-measures it live like a density change, the reader through its own size (`AppSettings.readerSize`: ⌘+ and ⌘− step it alone, kept across launches, reset by the setting), the quick reply, and a composer's subject and body, which `ComposeTextView` sets again in place; chrome and Settings stay at Default), themes (spec §4.16: a token that differs across themes carries one set of values per theme, with per-theme Graphite values, and a theme's `NSColor`s are made the first time it is drawn; `QuireEnvironment.setTheme` ends with one redraw sweep, every view of every window marked for display and token window backgrounds assigned again, because views that paint surfaces in `draw(_:)` otherwise redraw only on an appearance change; SwiftUI reads the theme as an environment value, `quireTheme`, which `QuireThemeRoot` sets on every hosting root and the generated `QuireColorStyle` shape styles resolve from, so Settings re-themes in place keeping identity, state and keyboard focus), `QuireLayerColors` (re-resolves `CGColor`s on appearance and environment changes), `Quire.Motion` (token animations, Reduce Motion fallbacks, `finishAll()`, and `QuireFrameAnimation` for a view that redraws at every step, stepped once a frame along the same curves), `QuireGlyph`/`QuireSVGPath` and `QuireSymbol`.
- **Reader CSS**: `QuireCSS.head` lives in SSCore because the reader shell is built there and SSCore does not import AppKit. It is byte-identical to the specimen's `<style>` head (`QuireCSSTests`). `QuireCSS.textSizes`, generated beside it, redefines every `--q-font-*` at the other Text sizes under `html.ss-text-small`, `-large` and `-larger`, and gives each role's step as `--q-step-<role>`, which the shell adds to the boxes it sizes around a sheet header's lines; the shell and every body document carry the reader's class (the payload's `textSize`, then `ss.preferences`, which measures the bodies again), so styled HTML, which never reads the variables, keeps its own sizes. The active theme's sheet, `QuireCSS.themeSheet(_:)` (the head's selectors holding only the tokens that differ from Study; empty for Study, at most about 7.7 KB), sits in one `<style id="ss-theme">` after the head, and every body document carries its cut, `ConversationShellAssets.bodyCut(_:)`, the same keep-list filter that cuts the body's variables from the head, so the two never disagree; `ss.setTheme` replaces both without reloading a frame and posts `themed`. The print document carries the head alone, so a printout is Study's light values in any theme.
- **Paper faces** (spec §5.55): the art is generated like the glyphs (`docs/design/source/paper_faces.json` → `tokens.json` → `QuireTokensPaperFaces.swift`: `Quire.Paper`, the papers, and `Quire.PaperFace`, each face's layers as SVG path data with a paper and a lift). `QuireAvatarRenderer` parses a face's paths once per process (`QuireSVGPath`) and draws each face once per diameter, backing scale and Light or Dark into a `CGImage` (the ground, then each cut-out with its lift's shadow, clipped to the disc), kept for the process's life in a small lock-protected table (at most the twelve faces at the five sizes the surfaces draw, in Light and Dark: under 2 MB at 2×, so nothing evicts it, unlike the monograms' NSCache); `paperFaceRenderCount` lets the tests prove a redraw renders nothing. AppKit surfaces draw it in `draw(_:)` (`drawAccountFace(_:slot:in:)`, `drawPaperFace(_:in:)`, which reads the backing scale from the context, so a 2× snapshot gets 2× pixels), and SwiftUI through `QuireAvatarView(account:ink:)` as an `Image` at the environment's `displayScale` (which debug snapshots set to `--snapshot-scale` with `SnapshotDisplayScale`, since the window's own is the screen's). The title menu draws a paper face only at 2× and up; on a 1× screen its 12 pt face would be 12 pixels, so it draws the monogram's first letter there. The papers follow Light and Dark only: no theme, Graphite or Increase Contrast changes them, so the key holds none of those.
- **Components**: `Sources/SSDesign/AppKit` holds the AppKit components: `QuireControl` and the controls built on it (`QuireButton`, `QuireIconButton`, `QuirePopUpButton`, `QuireCheckbox`, `QuireRadioGroupView`, `QuireToggle`, `QuireTextField` with its secure variant, `QuireSearchField`), `QuireBannerView`, the slips (`QuireSlipView` for toasts, `QuireUndoPanelView`, `QuireSequenceHUDView`), `QuireKeycap`, `QuireAvatarRenderer`, `QuireHairline`, and `QuireWindowChrome`, which configures and measures the main and composer windows' title bars. `Sources/SSDesign/SwiftUI` holds the Settings controls (`QuireButtonStyle`, `QuireFieldStyle`, `QuireSecureField`, `QuireRadioGroup`, `QuireBanner`, `QuireAccountRow`, `QuireFaceButton` and `QuireFacePicker` (the face picker card, §5.55) and the rest). The app composes them and defines no colours, fonts or metrics of its own.
- **Accent and icon**: `scripts/build-app.sh` compiles `Resources/Assets.xcassets` and `Resources/AppIcon.icon` into `Contents/Resources/Assets.car` with `xcrun actool` (Xcode), and sets `NSAccentColorName = AccentColor` and `CFBundleIconName = AppIcon`; the drawn `AppIcon.icns` is copied beside it as `CFBundleIconFile`. The system reads `CFBundleIconName` from `Assets.car` first on every supported macOS, so with the document compiled macOS 15 shows `actool`'s flat renderings of it (Liquid Glass's edge included, no pixel-fitted 16 and 32 px), and the `.icns` is the icon only for tools that open `CFBundleIconFile`; `actool` ignores an `AppIcon.appiconset` of the same name beside the document. Without actool it warns: the bundle falls back to the system accent and the `.icns` alone (no dark or tinted icon on macOS 26 and later). A macOS app icon set cannot carry dark or tinted images, which is why the icon is an Icon Composer document. The document holds the tile as its fill and the paper plane as one layer, each a solid colour specialized for dark and tinted (black and white only, glass, specular, shadow and translucency off); the system masks it and draws the rest. `app-icon.swift --preview` renders its six system appearances through Xcode's `ictool`.
- **Design Gallery** (`Sources/SuperSparrowApp/Design`, debug builds only): Debug › Design Gallery, or `--gallery`, opens every SSDesign component in every state on a page laid out like section D of `docs/design/specimen.html`, with a bar that forces the appearance, the font, the theme and the Quire environment (Graphite, Increase Contrast, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency) for that window. `--snapshot-gallery=<png>` renders the whole page. Its page furniture (`DesignGalleryStudio`) copies the specimen's, which is not a Quire token and appears on no other screen.
- **Tests**: `Tests/SSDesignTests` asserts every contrast pair in four modes (and every Graphite pair) in each of the five themes, the account-palette rules, the theme rules R0–R11 (spec §4.16), the radius scale, row metrics across scripts, the glyph paths, and that the runtime still equals `tokens.json`. The colour math is a port of `docs/design/source/colorlib.py`; a pair that fails in Swift but passes in python is a port bug.

## Launch options

Debug aids, read by `LaunchOptions` (and `--snapshot-state` by the main window). They never touch the user's mail: the welcome and alert snapshots run over a throwaway store, and the others need `--demo`. Every path is absolute. Only `--demo` and `--reset-demo` exist in release builds; a release build ignores every other option here, so nothing can make it write its windows (Settings shows the configured OAuth client ID) to a path a caller chooses. The Design Gallery (`Sources/SuperSparrowApp/Design`) is compiled only into debug builds. Snapshots of the main window, a reader window or a composer use the default frame, split, reader window and composer size, ignore the pointer, and never save a size. No snapshot reads or saves the account scope (`LaunchOptions.isSnapshot`): every window snapshot opens on All Inboxes and leaves the user's choice as it was. Nor does one ask or change which app opens mail links or the Mac's login items: Settings › General shows SuperSparrow as not the default and not opening at login, and a mailbox of 292 MB whatever the Mac holds.

Rules that keep the budgets: search observations fetch their first page off the main thread and deliver only when the rows change; fixed row heights; no Auto Layout inside list rows; attributed strings built once per row and cached by conversation id plus a version; avatars decoded off the main thread, initials painted first; account faces drawn from a bitmap cache keyed by face, size, scale and Light or Dark, so the list's account tile is one image draw per row and J/K stays inside its 16 ms; `DatabasePool` reads never wait on sync writes; sync writes batched: a changes page's upserts in one transaction and its deletes in another (up to 100 messages), its live marks committed first in their own (docs/DATA-MODEL.md, "Backfill marks"), and one transaction per listing page (100) or metadata chunk (10); no network on the launch path. Each live poll is a `sync.poll` signpost interval (subsystem `io.supersparrow.mac`, category `sync`), so new-mail latency can be read in Instruments. A body fetched as a message opens is `conversation.body`, with its `stored` event, and `sync.body` in the engine ("Opening a message"). A search is two intervals. `list.search` (subsystem `io.supersparrow.mac`, category `list`) runs from the moment the list takes a search query (the field's text, sent 120 ms after the last keystroke, or another account picked while searching) to the moment the list has applied that query's first page, so it is read against the search keystroke budget; a query replaced before its rows arrive (more typing, a cleared field, another view) ends its interval with the marker `superseded`, so at most one is open; a query whose store read fails ends it then with `failed` (the store reports a stopped observation to its subscriber), so a broken search never reads as a slow or superseded one; and a blank query opens none. `store.search` (category `store`) spans each search read in the store, words or operators (`ConversationTable.fetch`: off the main thread for an observed first page or refresh, on the caller's thread for a further page), so Instruments shows how much of the wait is SQLite. A search of the server is a `sync.serverSearch` interval (category `sync`) in the engine. A Text size change is two: `list.remeasure` (category `list`) spans the list's reload at the new row heights (a density change too), and `conversation.textSize` (category `conversation`) runs from telling the ready shells the reader's new size to the last of them having run it, which measures every open body again inside the script; the follow-up measure 50 ms later is left out. None carries the query text, a subject or an address.

An idle app keeps no timer faster than the poll, but for one that is not ours: while the app is active and analytics has started in the run, TelemetryDeck's SDK keeps a 1 s main-thread timer that records the session's length in its own defaults (2.x has no switch for it; it stops whenever the app goes behind, provided its session began in front, which is why the SDK starts only once the app is active; "Analytics"). Analytics' own send timer is 60 s. The storage sweeper and the size Settings shows keep none at all ("Storage bounds"), and nor does the snooze runner with nothing snoozed; with something snoozed it keeps one wait, to the soonest time, and while a due snooze waits for its account's check, one more of a minute at most ("Later"). The send queue and the sync line's sending count wait as "Send" describes. Each reader, a reader window's too, follows the outbox for its pending sheets and its watched quick replies through the store's observation (`observePendingSends`), only while one of them has something on its way, and none polls it. The sync line's "retrying in 30 s" is chosen again every second only while the line shows that count (Slow) in a window that is not occluded; behind Offline, Couldn't sync or Sign in required a retry ahead shows no count, and an outage keeps one ahead almost all the time, so those lines keep no timer, and a count in the tooltip alone is brought up to date as the pointer enters the line. Settings › Accounts ticks its clock only while its window is open and a row counts a retry down. The calendar lane keeps no timer: the live lane's cycles that catch up wake it, and it reads only when its last read is two minutes old in front or ten minutes old behind, or when asked ("Calendar"). The day column's and the week's now line moves once a minute (`dur.nowTick`) only while a calendar shows in a window on screen and not occluded, and the day turns on `NSCalendarDayChanged`, not on a timer. The reader's shell looks every 300 ms for focus moving from one body to another only while a body has focus and the page has it; the web view tells the shell when the page may have it again (becoming first responder, its window becoming key), since focus returning to a body raises no event the shell hears.

## Crash reports

Crashes, exceptions and hangs go to Sentry through its Cocoa SDK (`sentry-cocoa`, the static `Sentry` product; setup, testing and symbols in docs/ANALYTICS-AND-REPORTS.md). SuperSparrow writes no crash handling of its own: the SDK's handlers (Mach exceptions, signals, C++ and Objective-C exceptions) record a report to disk as the app dies and send it at the next launch, and its hang tracker, MetricKit diagnostics and sessions (the crash-free rate per release) send as they happen.

- **Start.** `main.swift` makes the application object, then calls `CrashReporting.start` before anything else of the app's, so a crash during launch is reported. Not earlier: making `NSApplication` sets AppKit's own uncaught-exception handler, which replaces the SDK's, and an exception thrown on a background queue would then reach Sentry as a bare SIGABRT, without its name or reason. Starting costs 7 to 9 ms on the main thread (debug build), before the window, and does not grow with the mailbox. It starts only with a DSN (the bundle's `SentryDSN`, written by `scripts/build-app.sh` from `SENTRY_DSN` or the untracked `Resources/SentryDSN`; `--sentry-dsn` in a debug build), while Settings › General › Analytics and reports is on (`sendsCrashReports`, the key it had when it was Crash reports, read from the defaults before the app delegate exists; on unless turned off; it governs "Analytics" too; turning it off closes the SDK at once and deletes its queue, kept in a folder of the app's own, `~/Library/Caches/io.supersparrow.mac/Sentry`, rather than the `io.sentry` and `SentryCrash` folders every unsandboxed app embedding the SDK shares; on starts it again), and never for a snapshot run, which a timeout may end. Tests never start it.
- **What a report holds.** The release (`io.supersparrow.mac@<version>+<build>`, the build as its distribution) and environment (`debug` or `production`), the Mac's model, macOS version, architecture, memory and locale, the app's state (active, uptime), every thread's stack as image offsets with the loaded images' UUIDs, the exception's name and reason or the signal, a `mode` tag (`demo` or `accounts`), the SDK's random installation id, and up to 100 breadcrumbs.
- **Breadcrumbs** are names from the code, never content: `CrashReporting.breadcrumb(_:_:)` takes a `StaticString`; every action the app sends is recorded by its selector (`SuperSparrowApplication.sendAction`); a window becoming key or closing by its controller's type, never its title (a reader window's title is a subject); and the app's life (`CrashBreadcrumbs`: launched, active, behind, the Mac sleeping and waking, memory pressure, quitting).
- **What never leaves.** The SDK's collectors that could see mail are off: automatic breadcrumbs (window titles), network breadcrumbs, failed-request events and tracing (Gmail and Graph URLs name messages and carry search words), memory introspection around a crash, source context, logs and metrics, and all swizzling. `sendDefaultPii` is off, so no IP address is inferred. `CrashReportScrubber` (the SDK's `beforeSend` and `beforeBreadcrumb`) then passes every string of a report (message, exception, tags, extras, contexts, frames, image paths, breadcrumbs) through `CrashRedactor`, which turns the home folder into `~`, any other `/Users/<name>` into `/Users/<user>`, anything shaped like an email address into `<address>` and the account's short name, as a word, into `<user>`, and it drops the Mac's name, the server name, the request and every user field but the installation id. Checked end to end against a local listener standing in for Sentry: the envelopes held no home path, account name, computer or host name, or address.
- **Exceptions AppKit catches.** AppKit catches an exception thrown while it dispatches an event on the main thread, logs it and goes on. `SuperSparrowApplication.reportException(_:)` reports it to Sentry as an error (tag `caught_by: appkit`, the throw site's symbols as `thrown_from`), and the app goes on as before. Sentry's own capture (`enableUncaughtNSExceptionReporting`, or its `SentryCrashExceptionApplication`) was not taken: it sets `NSApplicationCrashOnExceptions`, which would end the app at every such exception. An exception AppKit catches during layout or a Core Animation commit goes through its private `_crashOnException:`, which ends the app; the SDK sees the SIGABRT and the stack, not the reason, since catching it would mean the private method.
- **Hangs.** The main thread blocked for 2 s (`CrashReporting.appHangTimeout`) is reported once it answers again, with the main thread's stack; MetricKit adds the hangs, CPU and disk-write excess the system measured, at a later launch.
- **Symbols.** `scripts/build-app.sh` writes the build's dSYM beside the bundle (`dist/SuperSparrow.app.dSYM`, about a second with `dsymutil`); `scripts/upload-dsyms.sh` uploads it with Sentry's `sentry` CLI (or the legacy `sentry-cli`), so frames in the app read as functions and lines. Without it Sentry symbolicates the system's frames and shows the app's as offsets.

## Analytics

Which features are used, from where and how fast go to TelemetryDeck through its Swift SDK (`SwiftSDK`, the `TelemetryDeck` product, `from: "2.14.2"`; setup, the full catalog and the end-to-end check in docs/ANALYTICS-AND-REPORTS.md). It never sees mail: the app hands it signals from a closed catalog only.

- **Start.** `main.swift` calls `Analytics.start` right after `CrashReporting.start`. It takes signals from then, while Settings › General › Analytics and reports is on (`sendsCrashReports`, the switch crash reports already had, so an earlier opt-out holds), with an app ID (the bundle's `TelemetryDeckAppID`, written by `scripts/build-app.sh` from `TELEMETRYDECK_APP_ID` or the untracked `Resources/TelemetryDeckAppID`; `--telemetry-app-id` in a debug build), and never for a snapshot run. The SDK itself starts the first time the app is active (`NSApplication.didBecomeActiveNotification`), and the signals sent before then (at most 100) follow it: 2.x starts its session half a second after it starts, and a session begun behind other apps leaves its 1 s timer running for the run (it replaces the timer without stopping it when the app comes to the front). Tests never start it: they hand `Analytics` a recorder (`AnalyticsTransport`), and `TelemetryDeckTransport` a stand-in for the SDK. Debug builds send TelemetryDeck's test signals, which its dashboard keeps apart. The SDK sends what it has queued every 60 s, in batches, through an ephemeral `URLSession` (no cookies or cache on disk), and logs only its errors.
- **The catalog.** `AnalyticsSignal` is an enum: 24 signals (the launch, Settings opened and changed, mail actions, composing and sending, the quick reply, searches, reader windows, views and scopes moved to, the calendar, undo, accounts added and removed, Help me write's writes, and sampled timings), each with parameters from closed sets: enum raw values, counts in ranges (`2-5`) and durations in ranges (`16-50ms`). A call site can only choose a case; `AnalyticsTests` sends every case with every value and checks each against the vocabulary and the doc. Every signal also says `mode` (`demo` or `accounts`), the SDK's own included: the app hands it to the SDK as a default parameter (`Analytics.sdkParameters`, behind a lock, since the SDK may build a signal off the main thread).
- **Where a step came from.** `Analytics.trigger` is `key` while a key is being handled (a menu item's key equivalent, a key in a reader window), else what the way in declared with `Analytics.from(_:)` (a swipe, a drop, the reader's or the calendar's buttons, the rail, a toast's Undo, a notification, a reader window's menu items), else `menu`. A step the app takes on its own inside a user's path (the rail falling back to All Inboxes as an account goes) runs in `Analytics.quietly`, and sends nothing. The hooks sit on the paths every way in shares: `MainWindowController.mailAction` and `messageAction` (and the snooze, mute, sender-rule, unsubscribe and Later archive paths), `undo(groupID:)`, `navigate` around the user's view and scope picks, the composer's `present` functions (a window opened, not one brought forward) and sends, the list's search subscription and `ServerSearchController`, `openInNewWindow`, the calendar's toggles and `setAside`, `AppSettings`' setters and the At login and Mail links models, `AppDelegate`'s launch, Settings and account paths, and Help me write's milestones (`HelpMeWrite`, as `AIWriter` reports a write's end).
- **Timings.** Where the app keeps a signpost (`list.refresh`, `conversation.show`, `list.search`), one in twenty is sent, at most twenty of each an hour (so a run left open for days is measured all along), as a range and its milliseconds; a server search says how long it took. A local search counts once it has stood for 2 s, since the field searches at every keystroke; only its number of operators and of results are sent, never its words.
- **Who.** TelemetryDeck counts users by the SDK's own random identifier (made on first use, kept in its defaults suite `com.telemetrydeck.<12 hex digits>`), hashed with SHA-256 on the Mac and again on its server. SuperSparrow never sets the SDK's `defaultUser` or a `customUserID`, so no address or account name can become it. Checked end to end in the demo against a local listener standing in for TelemetryDeck (`--telemetry-url`, `--analytics-check`, 20 November 2026): the signals held none of the demo's addresses, names or subjects, nor the search's words, the user's or the Mac's name, and nothing arrived after the switch was turned off, nor after it was turned on again in the run, nor was a queue left on disk. The SDK adds its defaults to every signal (app version and build, macOS version, the Mac's model, screen, locale, region, languages and time zone, light or dark, two accessibility settings, the exact time and the hour and day, session counts), and each request shows the Mac's IP address and, in its headers, the app's name and version and the preferred languages; listed in docs/ANALYTICS-AND-REPORTS.md.
- **Off.** `Analytics.setEnabled(false)` stops at once: nothing more is sent from the app, the SDK is set `analyticsDisabled` and dropped (`TelemetryDeck.terminate`), with what it held in memory; a request already on its way finishes. Then SuperSparrow's own signals are deleted from the SDK's queue file (`~/Library/Caches/telemetrysignalcache`, written only as the app quits, and shared by name with every unsandboxed app embedding the SDK, so other apps' entries stay) and the SDK's defaults suite is deleted, and again 1.5 s later, after any write of its session counts begun before the drop. On again in the same run, analytics waits for the next launch (crash reports start at once): the SDK keeps the run's sessions in a process-wide `SessionManager` that `terminate` does not reset and whose timer keeps counting, so a restart would send them, and write them back, under the new identifier. A launch that finds the switch off deletes what an earlier run left for the build's app ID and the one last started (`analyticsStartedAppID` in the app's defaults), and a build sending to another app ID, or none, deletes the last one's. Two more of the SDK's habits are worked around in `TelemetryDeckTransport`: `TelemetryDeck.signal` and the session the SDK starts 0.5 s after `initialize` reach for its shared manager, which traps in a debug build and, in a release build, is remade with an empty app ID if `terminate` has dropped it, so a stop in that first half second drops the SDK once its session has started (or as the app quits, after the SDK has written its queue, which is then deleted); and `initialize` reads the whole queue file, every app's signals in it, and sends them all, and its write at quit replaces the file, so other apps' entries are set aside while it reads and put back after, and those the file holds as the app quits go back after that write.
- **Cost.** Starting reads the queue file (usually absent) on the main thread, about 0.3 ms (release, M4 Pro, November 2026); a signal costs the main thread about 0.1 ms and is built off it. While the app is active the SDK keeps a 1 s main-thread timer (see "Performance budgets"); switched off, it keeps ticking but writes nothing. Switching to another app within half a second of SuperSparrow first coming to the front can still leave the timer running for the run; the fix belongs in the SDK.

## AI

Help me write (spec §5.56) is the one AI feature: a draft of the reply the user is writing, made only when they press Write, in place in the field they are writing in, by the provider they chose in Settings › AI. Off by default. The UI never calls a provider: `WriteSession` writes through `AIWriter` (SSCore), which resolves the provider through a closure the environment gives it (`AIProviderFactory.provider(for:)` in the app, the demo's writer in the demo and the tests), checks the user's permission again, runs one write per text field, enforces the 120 s deadline and turns Stop into a clean end, and maps every failure to an `AIError`.

- **Nothing reaches Anthropic or OpenAI unless the user acts.** The only contacts are: ⌘J, the ghost, the pill or the text's menu, the prompt line's ↩ or a chip, Try again or Shorter (the request, and for Claude Code or Codex the version and sign-in check right before it); Settings › AI's Check, or ↩ on a key just typed (the provider's models list with the key and nothing else, or the CLI's version and sign-in; a key saved as the field loses focus, Settings closes or the app quits is only saved); and the debug-only live check. Never at launch, on opening Settings or its AI tab, on choosing a provider (its privacy sheet is local), on typing notes (the ghost, the pill, the prompt line opening and the text's menu are local), on selecting or opening a conversation or a composer, on a timer, on wake, on quit, in the background or to prewarm. One gate in code: every provider request and every CLI launch takes an `AIUserAction`, which only the user-action handlers make (`WriteSession.start`, `AISettingsModel`'s `check` and `commitKey` on ↩), and goes through one place that records it against that action (`AIEgress`) before it leaves: `ProviderHTTP`, the only SSAI code that calls a `URLSession` send, and `CLIProcessRunner.run`, the only maker of a `CLIProcess`, the only code that starts a process. `HelpMeWriteEgressTests` scans every module for it, comments left out: the token made in any spelling (`.init(userPressed:)` included) only in those three functions, no extension adding another way, no `URLSession` send in SSAI outside `ProviderHTTP`, the providers' hosts named nowhere outside their two files, and no process started outside `CLIProcess`. On shutdown `AIWriter` cancels a Check under way and answers any later one from this Mac. The status Settings shows as it opens and whether the text shows Help me write's marks come from this Mac alone (`AIProvider.localHealth`): a key's presence (its Keychain attributes, no prompt), the CLI file found and executable, and its version as an earlier check found it or as its real path names it (~/.local/bin/claude leads to …/versions/2.1.322; Homebrew's codex to …/Caskroom/codex/0.163.0); `--version` is a launch, so it runs only on Check or Write, as `claude auth status` and `codex login status` (which may refresh a sign-in over the network) do. A sign-in found missing at send time is the write's inline error. `HelpMeWriteEgressTests` proves it with the real providers, a recording `URLProtocol` and a fake `claude` that logs each launch: launch (with no provider, with Claude Code turned on, with Claude's API and a stored key), Settings › AI opened and reopened, choosing a provider, typing, the ghost, the pill, the prompt line, the text's menu, switching conversations, a composer, a key typed and left, and quitting as the app does (`AppDelegate.prepareToQuit`, Settings open, a key typed) make none; each allowed action exactly its own.
- **Providers.** Anthropic's Messages API and OpenAI's Responses API, streamed over an ephemeral `URLSession` (no cache, no cookies, no waiting for a network that is not there), with the user's API key read from the Keychain only as a request is about to go (reading it can show the Keychain's prompt after a rebuild; Settings knows a key is there from its attributes alone). A failure before any text (429, 529, a 5xx, a lost connection, an overloaded or failed stream) is retried once, after the server's retry-after when that is 10 s or less; nothing is retried once text has arrived. The user's own Claude Code and Codex, run as a process per request (`CLIProcessRunner`): the one chosen in Settings while it is an executable file, else the first in ~/.local/bin, /opt/homebrew/bin and /usr/local/bin (Settings › AI shows the same one, through the runner's `locate`), never through a login shell; a version outside the tested range is refused with the version found, worded for which side it is on (too old: update it; newer: updating cannot help), and the sign-in is checked before every send, since a signed-out Codex still uploads the prompt; the request on stdin, never in the arguments, which `ps` shows; a new empty 0700 folder as its working directory, removed after; a minimal environment without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN`, `OPENAI_API_KEY` or `CODEX_API_KEY`, so the user's own sign-in is used; idle (30 s) and total (120 s) timeouts, then SIGTERM and SIGKILL 2 s later, sent to the CLI's whole process group so a helper it started ends with it, and on quit every CLI still running is ended before the app goes (`CLIProcessRunner.endAll`). Claude Code runs with every tool, MCP server, slash command and session file off and none of the user's, project's or local settings files (`--restricted --setting-sources ""`, its sign-in check too), so no `env` block, key helper, hook or plugin of the user's applies; it must report a claude.ai sign-in to Anthropic's own API (`firstParty`), and a run whose first line names an API key or a plugin of the user's ends there. Codex is refused for now (`AIError.toolsStayOn`): 0.163, with every tool feature disabled, still offers the model code mode's `exec`, `request_user_input` and the multi-agent tools (its request captured against a loopback server, no model call), so no version in the tested range runs until one is shown to offer none; its recipe and parser (which ends a run at any item other than the message, reasoning or a notice) stay ready for that version. A CLI process lives only for a request the user made: it is not a helper. The demo and the app's tests write through `DemoAIProvider`, which streams one of the demo's canned replies and sends nothing anywhere; `--live-ai-clis` (debug) points the demo at the real CLIs.
- **Permission, in Settings.** Choosing a provider in Settings › AI shows its privacy sheet at that moment (§5.33, §5.40: what goes, when, to whom; Cancel the default, which puts the popup back); Turn On stores the permission per provider in `AppSettings` (`aiConsent`, the demo's apart in `aiConsent.demo`, and a demo CLI run for real with `--live-ai-clis` apart again in `aiConsent.demo.live`, so a Turn On on the demo's “Nothing is sent” sheet never stands for a send), and choosing Off, or another provider turned on in its place, withdraws it. Nothing is asked while writing: a provider chosen before 1.12 and never turned on shows no ghost and no pill, ⌘J says so (“Turn it on in Settings › AI”) under the caret's line with Settings…, and Settings › AI offers Turn On…. `AIWriter` refuses without it (`AIError.consentRequired`), so no path sends without a Turn On.
- **What is sent.** On Write, `ReplyContextBuilder` runs off the main thread, after `MailClient.keepBodies(of:)`, over the whole conversation (drafts and the user's unsent text left out), newest first, weighed by age within about 24,000 characters: the message replied to (the latest, or the one the reply targets) in full up to 45% of the budget, cut with “ […]” past it; the next two newest in full up to 15% each; every older message as a digest built here, with no model call: its own words (quoted history stripped by `QuotedHistory`; its greeting, an opening pleasantry, a mail system's banner, “Sent from …”, the sign-off and the card under it and a disclaimer dropped by `MailBoilerplate`), its first whole sentences on one line up to a cap that starts at 400 characters and shrinks by 15% with each step back, to 80, every cap and the floor grown in proportion (up to three times) into what the messages sent whole left of the budget; and when even the digests overflow, the oldest fold into one line, “N earlier messages from A, B and C, about …” (their four most used words). A summary leads: the subject, the participants (names only, the user as “you”), the number of messages and their date span, and the question sentences of the message replied to. Each message is labelled with its real place (Latest, 2nd latest, …, a digest too, so one newer than an older message replied to never reads as older), its author's name and its date; a message sent whole keeps its greeting and sign-off name but not the card under it (title, phone, address) or a disclaimer; attachments are a count; the user's first name comes from their own messages, the name the app knows or a Microsoft account's display name (never a header another sender wrote, and letters only); their notes or selection and the instruction go as typed; a selection or notes longer than 8,000 characters are refused whole rather than cut, since the reply replaces them. Only each message's head is read (its share's characters, four bytes each at most, then cut back to a whole word, so the redactor never sees half an address), the fold's from a few words of each, and the summary's question from the message replied to as it is sent: a 40-message thread with a 30 KB message builds in a few milliseconds, 300 messages of 2,000 characters in a few tens. `AIRedactor` turns addresses (internationalised ones too) into names or “an address at contoso.com”, links into “[link: domain]”, one-time codes near a code word (digits in up to three groups; beside a word such as “verification”, “one-time”, “sign-in code” or “code is”, any five to ten letters and digits with a digit among them, one dash at most; Chinese, Japanese and Korean code words too) into “[code]” and card numbers into “[card number]”; every pattern is bounded, so a crafted mail costs time in proportion to its length. `WritePrompt` (`write-reply-2`) fences the mail with a per-request code, tells the model it is data, and describes the layout: newest first, weigh the latest most, messages newer than an older one replied to may update it, shortened older ones are background, reply from the user's side to the message replied to, the notes are the brief, a selection is rewritten alone, never invent facts. The API providers send the fixed instructions and that one user turn, nothing else. Claude Code adds its own to the request, none of it mail: a line naming the Agent SDK and a billing line with its version, an environment block (its working folder, which is the run's empty `ss-ai-` folder and names no user, whether that is a git repository, platform, shell, OS version, the model and today's date) and its session metadata (a device id hash and a session id); `--exclude-dynamic-system-prompt-sections` cannot drop the block, since 2.1.322 ignores it beside `--system-prompt` (captured against a loopback server with outbound network denied, no model call). OpenAI requests carry `store: false`.
- **Into the field, at the caret.** No card and nothing in the bars: everything is drawn in the reply's text by `WriteInlineLayer`, subviews of the text view placed from its layout manager (the caret's insertion rect, its line, its baseline), once per keystroke or frame and in well under 1 ms on a 2,000-word body (signpost `ai.place`, gated `HelpMeWritePerformanceTests`); the selection pill floats in a host view (the quick reply's bar, the composer's body page) and is placed only where it lies in the text's visible area. ⌘J (or the ghost, the pill, the text's menu) starts at once, from the notes, the thread alone, or the selection; ⇧⌘J opens the prompt line under the caret's line (its field and three chips; ↩ or a chip runs it and closes it; it keeps the instruction until the reply is sent). The prompt line and the afterline are reserved blocks: a full-width `NSTextContainer.exclusionPaths` rect under their anchor's line, so the lines below move down and nothing covers text, measured again only when a block opens, closes or the width or Text size changes; where the anchor is the last line the block hangs below the text and the host grows by its `overflow` (`ComposeTextView.reservedBottom`, the quick reply's field, beyond its five lines). The text storage never changes for a block. The reply streams into the text view in pencil (`ink2`) at most once a frame (`WriteSession`'s flush, signposted `ai.flush`) while the field refuses the user's own edits, with undo registration off; the end is one undoable change in ink, “Help Me Write”, from the notes to the reply (a rewrite keeps the selection's own leading and trailing space and line breaks), whose undo puts the caret back where it was before ⌘J and whose redo puts it after the reply (`WriteStreamEdit.commit` registers the caret on both sides of the text view's own step), so neither leaves the text selected for ⌘J to rewrite. Right after, and after ⌘Z then ⇧⌘Z, ⌘J tries again from the same notes: the reply's step is undone first (so the new reply's one step goes back to the notes) and the old reply stays in sight, in pencil, until the new words come; once the user edits the draft, ⌘J writes from what the field holds. Stop, Esc, a failure or a writer stopped from outside (quit, Leave Demo) put back exactly what was there, selection included; a failure's reason shows under the caret's line in the afterline (`danger`, Try again, Settings… when the fix is there). Until the first words come, what the reply will replace is drawn in pencil with a layout manager's temporary colour, not written: undo registration turned off in the same event as the user's typing would leave that typing's undo group open for good. While a reply streams, Edit › Undo and Redo wait in that window, and nothing takes the half-written reply: Send, ⌘↩, Send and Archive and Send Later are off in the quick reply and the composer, the composer's From and other fields, Discard Draft…, Insert Picture…, the format row and Add Link… wait (Discard also puts the body back before it keeps what Undo restores), a drop to attach on the quick reply is refused (`QuickReplyView.acceptsDrops`: it would move the half-written reply to a composer; a composer's attachment strip still takes files, dropped, pasted or from Attach Files…), and a composer whose send is held for dropped attachments starts no write, and any other change to the quick reply's text (a restored or parked draft, a clear, a send) ends Help me write there first, as does the bar going out of sight (the week, a composer taking the reply). A write's end moves the keyboard only when it was still in the text or the prompt line (`WriteSession.keyboardIsHere`), so keys typed elsewhere meanwhile stay there; the keyboard leaving the reply ends the afterline and closes the prompt line. On shutdown `AIWriter.shutDown()` refuses any write still on its way (its context being built) and `CLIProcessRunner.endAll` closes the runner, so no CLI starts after the app has ended its CLIs. A composer's draft is kept as typing keeps it.
- **What never leaves, or is never kept.** Prompts, mail, model output, CLI output and API keys are never logged above debug, never put in an error (`AIError`'s words are fixed text plus numbers) and never in a breadcrumb: `CrashReporting.Category.ai` takes the milestones (“write started”, “write finished”, “write failed”, “write stopped”) and the provider's kind as `StaticString`s, and ⌘J and ⇧⌘J reach Sentry only as their selectors, `helpMeWrite:` and `helpMeWriteWithInstructions:`. Analytics hears the same milestones as each write ends (`HelpMeWrite.used`: the provider's kind and `finished`, `stopped` or `failed`, nothing else), except for a reply the demo makes up on this Mac, which sends nothing (`HelpMeWrite.analyticsSignal`). API keys live in the Keychain (service “SuperSparrow AI”, account the provider's kind), never in the defaults, and Settings never shows a stored key.

## Security and privacy

- Tokens live in the Keychain. The database lives in `~/Library/Application Support/SuperSparrow/mail.sqlite` (0600).
- Removing an account removes its tokens through the token provider (`AccessTokenProvider.removeTokens`) before the Keychain item: a refresh still in flight is cancelled and its reply dropped, and for the rest of the run nothing saves that account's tokens again (a refresh, a reconnect whose browser step finishes after the removal; `reconnect` also checks the account is still stored before and after the sign-in), so a removed mailbox's refresh token never comes back to the Keychain.
- Adding an account whose address is already here reuses that account only when it is the same mailbox: for Microsoft, a work and a personal account can share an address, so the type each grant was signed in as decides, and for a grant from before the type was chosen (no tenant) the mailbox ids do (Graph's `/me` `id`, `AccountProfile.userID`, read with each grant); when either cannot be read they count as two accounts, since a duplicate can be removed where one account syncing the other's mailbox cannot be seen. A Keychain read that fails fails the add rather than merging.
- After a reconnect stores a fresh grant, the invalidation the parked lane sends on the reconnect's own wake does not throw it away for a refresh (a grant no request has used cannot have been refused), and Sync Now on an account parked for a refused Keychain does not invalidate at all (no token was cached): each saves a token request and Keychain reads, and with them prompts.
- Remote images are blocked by default; loading them is per message, and loaded ones are cached on disk. The "Load remote images" preference is read for every body synced and by the reader, which renders each body that has not had its images loaded under a Content-Security-Policy (`img-src ss-cid: data:`, no other fetches), so turning the preference off takes effect at once, even for bodies stored while it was on. Images the user lets load come over https or plain http alike: WebKit holds the reader's loads to the bundle's App Transport Security policy, which exempts web content only (`NSAllowsArbitraryLoadsInWebContent` in `Resources/Info.plist`), so a sender's http image is not refused (-1022) once allowed, while OAuth, Gmail, Graph and unsubscribe requests keep requiring TLS. A printout follows the reader: it fetches only the images the reader shows, under the same policy for the rest ("Print"). Find in conversation keeps to the same rules: it reads unopened bodies through `DOMParser`, whose document runs nothing and loads nothing, and in a frame adds only its marks, so the sandbox and the policy hold.
- Open in Calendar writes an invitation's calendar text to a per-run temporary folder and hands it to Calendar; SuperSparrow reads and writes no calendar on the Mac (no EventKit). Answering an invitation and showing the calendar need the provider's calendar scope, asked for only when the user first answers or presses Connect under the day column; with it the engine reads each account's main calendar for the window it keeps and writes there only answers and the time set aside for a mail, busy and private (whoever else sees the calendar sees only that the time is busy, never the mail's subject), with no guests, so nobody is mailed about it.
- Attachments are written to a per-session temporary folder and quarantined as they are written (`com.apple.quarantine`, type email attachment, agent SuperSparrow), so Gatekeeper asks before anything in them runs; copies saved to Downloads (Download, Download all, Save to Downloads, Save Attachments…) or with Save As keep the mark, and a copy that has lost it is marked again; one that cannot be marked (a volume without extended attributes) is removed and reported as not saved. A file dragged out to the Finder or another app is marked before it is given its name, and never replaces a file already there ("Rendering"). Opening a program or script (`.app`, `.command`, `.terminal`, `.jar`, `.pkg`, shell and AppleScripts, `.fileloc`) from the reader asks first, with Cancel as the default. Errors about these files are logged by domain and code only, since a file error names the attachment.
- Crash reports go to Sentry ("Crash reports") and analytics to TelemetryDeck ("Analytics"), with nothing in either that a user wrote or received: no mail, subjects, addresses, account names, search words, file names or URLs. One switch, Settings › General › Analytics and reports, turns both off at once and deletes what they had queued. Email content goes only to Google, Microsoft, this Mac and, only when the user presses Write in Help me write, the AI provider they turned on in Settings › AI past its privacy sheet (Anthropic or OpenAI, directly with their API key or through their own Claude Code or Codex): the conversation they are replying to, weighed, shortened and redacted ("AI"). Nothing reaches that provider, and no CLI of theirs starts, unless they press Write or Check (or ↩ on a key they typed). Help me write is off by default. API keys for it live in the Keychain.
- Unsubscribe and remote images the user chose to load are the only requests that reach a sender's servers. A one-click request is a POST to the sender's own server from this Mac (it carries the URI the sender chose, which can identify the user, and nothing else: no cookies, no credentials), and a page opens in the user's browser. A mailto request is mail sent from the user's account through Google or Microsoft. The URI is never logged above debug ("Unsubscribe").
